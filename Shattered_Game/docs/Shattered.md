# Shattered — Vision Reference

## 1. One-Sentence Pitch

**Shattered** is a dark isometric online life-skill MMO where players shape their own personal island, explore a living dead-god archipelago, trade with others, craft meaningful goods, and experience a world that evolves through player actions and changing worldstates.

## 2. Core Identity

The game combines:

- **Old School RuneScape-style progression**: long-term skills, player economy, slow mastery, open-ended goals, stable level caps.
- **Stardew Valley-style personal space**: a customizable home island that expresses identity, creativity, function, and personal rhythm.
- **Deliberate isometric combat**: slow, readable, intense fights focused on 1v1 or small-group encounters rather than hack-and-slash mob clearing.
- **Living MMO world**: shared harbour towns, player markets, public events, co-op ruins, evolving island states, and quests affected by the world condition.
- **Elden Ring combat like**: the feeling of dodge danger and reacton in battle

The game should feel like:

> “This is my island in a living world.”

## 3. Target Feeling

The game should feel:

- Cozy, but not childish.
- Dangerous, but not constantly stressful.
- Social, but not forced.
- Deep, but easy to understand.
- Slow and meaningful, not grindy for the sake of grind.
- MMO-like, but not dependent on massive player counts everywhere.
- A gameloop that works from the first second, gets deeper over time but not more complex

The emotional contrast is important:

- **Home island:** personal, creative, safe, expressive.
- **Shared harbour:** social, active, full of trade and life.
- **Outer islands:** strange, dangerous, mysterious, rewarding.
- **Ruins/dungeons:** tense, slower, tactical, atmospheric.

## 4. World Premise

The world is an oceanic archipelago called **The Wake**.

Long ago, the gods died. Their bodies, memories, bones, organs, dreams, and divine remains became islands. Civilization now survives across these strange islands.

Players are **Wanderer**: people who reclaim small abandoned islands and turn them into homes, farms, workshops, shrines, markets, gardens, docks, and personal sanctuaries.

The world is not a generic fantasy continent. It is an ocean of weird, beautiful, dangerous dead-god islands.

Example island themes:

- **Lungwood Isle:** breathing forests, rare herbs, living trees, poison beasts.
- **Spinebreak Atoll:** bone cliffs, mining, ancient ore, buried giants.
- **Saltveil Marsh:** fishing, drowned ruins, ghost crabs, alchemy materials.
- **The Emberjaw:** volcanic island, smithing materials, fire creatures, molten ruins.
- **Moonroot Keys:** magical crops, farming, night-blooming plants.
- **Choir Reef:** siren cults, sound-based bosses, coral ruins, cursed songs.

## 5. The Three Equal Play Paths

The game has three main play paths.

None of these paths should be mandatory.
Each path must be playable as a complete game by itself.
The paths should create synergy through the economy, worldstate, and player cooperation.

### Path A — Homestead / Island Building

Players can focus mainly on their personal island.

They should be able to:

- Terraform and shape the island layout.
- Place buildings freely.
- Design paths, fields, docks, bridges, gardens, walls, rooms, and interiors.
- Choose the island’s purpose and style.
- Build functional modules like farms, shops, inns, shrines, workshops, greenhouses, fishing ponds, museums, or guest areas.
- Host visitors and friends.
- Run island services.
- Participate in festivals, island ratings, decoration events, and town projects.
- Progress without needing to fight or deeply craft everything themselves.

The island should be fully personal, not a fixed farm layout.

The island is not just decoration. It is the player’s identity.

Possible island specializations:

- Farm island
- Forge island
- Fishing island
- Alchemy island
- Shrine island
- Market island
- Museum island
- Inn island
- Guild meeting island
- Decorative showcase island

### Path B — Exploration / Combat

Players can focus mainly on travelling, questing, fighting, discovering, and clearing dangerous places.

They should be able to:

- Use a simple basic home island without needing to deeply build.
- Buy food, potions, tools, and gear from other players or NPCs.
- Travel through shared islands and dangerous zones.
- Discover ruins, secrets, lore fragments, rare resources, and bosses.
- Fight monsters in slow, tactical, readable encounters.
- Join small co-op dungeons or world events.
- Bring back rare materials, trophies, relics, seeds, maps, and resources for the economy.
- Affect the worldstate through discoveries, cleared threats, and quest outcomes.
- Go on treasure hunts, find clues and do quests given by other players or the world/ npcs

Combat players should not be forced to farm or decorate.

### Path C — Crafting / Economy

Players can focus mainly on production, trade, contracts, refining, and economic gameplay.

They should be able to:

- Buy materials from explorers and gatherers.
- Create useful goods for other players.
- Specialize in crafting professions.
- Fulfil player contracts.
- Run shops or market stalls.
- Become known for specific goods.
- Progress without needing to fight bosses or build a complex island.

Possible crafting specializations:

- Smithing
- Cooking
- Alchemy
- Carpentry
- Tailoring
- Furniture
- Shipbuilding
- Tools
- Weapons
- Potions
- Food buffs
- Relic restoration
- Island modules
- Decorative objects

Crafters should be socially and economically important.

## 6. Core Design Rule

The game should never say:

> “You must play this path to progress.”

Instead, it should say:

> “You can do this yourself, trade with others, hire services, join friends, or take another route.”

No single path should fully dominate progression.

Combat should not be the only source of key resources.
Crafting should not be mandatory for basic play.
Base-building should not be required for combat power.
The market should connect all three paths.

## 7. Player Economy

The economy is the bridge between the three paths.

Each path produces things the others want:

- Builders create useful spaces, services, public islands, shops, farms, inns, shrines, and social value.
- Explorers bring back monster drops, relics, rare materials, maps, seeds, trophies, and discoveries.
- Crafters transform resources into gear, tools, food, potions, furniture, building modules, and trade goods.
- The world itself also pushes the economy, with demand of items and pricing changing from day to day, safety of the location, amount of monsters and how saturated the market is right now

Players should be able to specialize (But one path does not look them out of another).

A pure explorer can sell rare loot and buy food/gear.
A pure crafter can buy materials and sell finished goods.
A pure builder can buy materials and earn through services, shops, farming, hosting, decoration, and island functions.

The economy should make players need each other without forcing them into unwanted gameplay.

## 8. Progression Philosophy

The game uses **evergreen progression with capped vertical power**.

It should avoid the World of Warcraft-style expansion treadmill where every expansion raises the level cap and invalidates old content.

The preferred model is closer to OSRS:

- Skill caps remain stable.
- Old content remains useful.
- New content adds sideways depth.
- New islands add new resources, quests, recipes, mechanics, styles, and stories.
- Players keep long-term progress meaningful.

New content should expand the world, not reset it.

Good new content:

- New island
- New profession branch
- New recipe chain
- New boss mechanics
- New crop types
- New furniture style
- New faction
- New sailing route
- New public event
- New quest arc
- New island module
- New cosmetic identity
- New economic demand

Bad new content:

- Higher level cap every expansion
- Old gear becomes worthless
- Players are forced into the newest zone only
- Previous skills become irrelevant
- Power creep invalidates old progression

## 9. Worldstate System

The world should feel alive and evolving.

Each major shared island can have a worldstate with values such as:

- Corruption
- Prosperity
- Faction influence
- Monster pressure
- Resource abundance
- Weather
- Season
- Trade demand
- Discovered ruins
- Public event status
- Route safety
- NPC presence

These values affect:

- Which quests appear
- Which resources are available
- Which monsters spawn
- Which NPCs appear or disappear
- Market demand
- Public events
- World boss conditions
- Environmental visuals
- Travel safety
- Island music and atmosphere

The world should not be fully random. It should use authored content pieces triggered dynamically by worldstate.

## 10. Quest Philosophy

Quests should feel authored, meaningful, and responsive.

Avoid infinite generic AI-generated quests.

The ideal quest system is:

> Handcrafted quest pieces + dynamic worldstate triggers + multiple valid solution paths.

A quest may appear because of a changing world condition.
Different player types should be able to contribute in different ways.

Example quest:

**Restore the Saltveil Lighthouse**

Trigger conditions:

- Storm season is active.
- Sea route safety is low.
- Corruption is above 40%.
- The lighthouse has not been restored.

Possible contribution paths:

- Combat players clear sea monsters.
- Crafters repair the lens and supply materials.
- Builders provide housing or infrastructure support.
- Traders deliver oil, rope, glass, and food.
- Explorers recover the lost keeper’s journal.

Outcome:

- If restored, the route becomes safer, traders return, new quests unlock, and certain goods become cheaper.
- If ignored, storms worsen, monsters increase, prices rise, and a world boss may appear.

## 11. Personal Island System

Every player owns a personal island.

The island must be highly customizable.

Players should be able to control:

- Terrain shape
- Building placement
- Room layout
- Paths
- Bridges
- Fields
- Gardens
- Docks
- Water features
- Decoration
- Functional modules
- Visitor access
- Shop access
- Public/private areas
- Island theme and purpose

The island should support both aesthetics and function.

Functional island modules may include:

- Farm fields
- Greenhouse
- Forge
- Workshop
- Kitchen
- Alchemy lab
- Shrine
- Fishing pond
- Market stall
- Shopfront
- Inn
- Guest house
- Trophy hall
- Museum
- Stable
- Dock
- Ferry point
- Guild meeting space
- Storage cellar
- Festival area

The base should never feel like a mandatory chore.
It should feel like ownership, creativity, progression, and identity.

## 12. Combat Philosophy

Combat should be important, but it should not dominate the entire game.

The combat should be:

- Isometric
- Slow
- Intense
- Readable
- Weapon-based
- Stamina-based
- Encounter-focused
- Low hotbar
- Mostly 1v1
- Sometimes 1v2–3
- Max 1v4–5 for weaker mobs
- Focused on preparation, timing, spacing, and enemy patterns

Combat should not be hack-and-slash.
Combat should not be about clearing huge screens of enemies.
Combat should not feel like late-game Path of Exile.

A good fight should feel like:

> “I need to read this enemy, manage stamina, choose the right moment, and avoid getting surrounded.”

A group of three enemies should already feel dangerous.
Five enemies should usually mean the player made a risky decision.

## 13. Combat Loop

The combat loop is:

1. Observe the enemy.
2. Prepare with buffs, tools, weapon, or positioning.
3. Engage carefully.
4. Read enemy attacks.
5. Dodge, block, or reposition.
6. Punish openings.
7. Recover stamina.
8. Decide whether to continue, retreat, or push deeper.

Combat should reward:

- Patience
- Spacing
- Timing
- Preparation
- Knowledge
- Terrain use
- Weapon mastery
- Calm decision-making

Combat should punish:

- Button mashing
- Getting surrounded
- Greedy attacks
- Ignoring stamina
- Ignoring enemy tells
- Entering dangerous areas unprepared

## 14. Combat Controls

Possible control scheme:

- WASD: movement
- Mouse: aim / target / interact
- Left click: basic attack
- Right click: weapon skill or heavy attack
- Space: dodge / step
- Shift: block, stance, or aim modifier
- Q / E: utility abilities
- 1–4: food, potion, tool, or quick item

Keep the hotbar small.
The depth should come from combat decisions, not dozens of ability buttons.

## 15. Weapon Identity

Weapons define playstyle.
There are no hard combat classes.

Possible weapon styles:

### Sword 

Balanced, defensive, safe for beginners.
Good blocking and countering.

### Spear

Control and range.
Good against beasts, charges, and single targets.
Weak when surrounded.

### Hammer

Slow and heavy.
High stagger and armor break.
Strong against bosses and armored enemies.
High stamina cost.

### Bow

Positioning and preparation.
Good for pulling enemies, hunting, and weak points.
Not a machine-gun weapon.
Weak up close.

### Dagger

Fast and risky.
Bleed, poison, mobility, backstabs.
Punishing if mistimed.


## 17. Death and Failure

Failure should matter, but should not be cruel.

Avoid full loot loss for normal play.

Possible failure consequences:

- Gear durability damage
- Temporary injury
- Dropped expedition loot only
- Curse buildup
- Respawn at nearest shrine
- Lost time/resources
- Failed contract
- Worldstate consequence during certain events

Hardcore/high-risk zones may have harsher rules, but they should be optional.

## 18. Multiplayer Structure

The player-facing goal is a seamless overworld: **The Wake should feel like one continuous OSRS-style worldmap**.

That does **not** mean one giant always-loaded runtime object. Internally, the overworld should be split into authored regions and streamed chunks/cells, with sparse changed world state.

Recommended structure:

### Seamless Overworld

- One continuous world coordinate space for players
- Internally split into regions/chunks/cells
- Shared public travel, gathering, events, NPCs, contracts, routes, and worldstate
- Streamed or materialized around the player
- Sparse changed chunk state rather than one giant world blob

### Personal Island

- Player-owned space
- Persistent layout/buildings later
- Can still be instanced or specially hosted
- Friends can visit
- Shops and services can operate from islands

### Special Instances

- Dungeons
- Boss arenas
- Interiors if needed
- Quest spaces
- Private edit/build spaces if needed

### Guild Islands / Outposts

- Later feature
- Shared group projects
- Social identity
- Large-scale crafting, trade, or event preparation

## 19. MMO Feeling

The MMO feeling should come from:

- Seeing other players in towns
- Trading with real players
- Player-run shops
- Shared markets
- Public events
- Seasonal festivals
- Co-op ruins
- Worldstate changes caused by players
- Player reputation
- Specialized roles
- Visiting player islands
- Guild projects
- Long-term server identity

The MMO feeling should not depend only on huge battles.

Small-scale combat can exist inside a large-scale social world.


## Visual Style

The game should use a **2.5D isometric style** with a fixed camera angle.

The goal is not full 3D realism and not flat top-down. The best direction is:

**isometric pixel art / painted pixel hybrid with modern lighting**

The style should support two moods:

- **Cozy and warm** on personal islands, farms, homes, harbours and social areas.
- **Strange and dangerous** on wild islands, ruins, cursed zones and boss areas.

The player’s island should feel like a real personal place, not just a menu or decoration screen. Isometric view helps make houses, docks, farms, paths, shops, gardens and shrines feel spatial and meaningful.

The shared harbour towns should feel readable and social. Players should be able to see each other, show off gear, gather at markets, meet for groups and visually understand what is happening without UI overload.

Combat must stay visually clean. The game should avoid Path of Exile-style screen spam. Since combat is slower and encounter-based, every enemy animation, attack direction and danger zone must be readable.

## Visual Rules

- Fixed isometric camera.
- Grid-based world underneath.
- Fake height through layers, shadows, cliffs, stairs and bridges.
- Strong silhouettes for players, enemies, buildings and interactable objects.
- Clear animation wind-ups for enemy attacks.
- Minimal combat clutter.
- Warm lighting for home/social areas.
- Darker, moodier lighting for dangerous areas.
- Calm, readable UI.
- No full 3D camera rotation.
- No chaotic hack-and-slash effects.

The game should feel charming and readable first, detailed second.

## Technical Build Direction

The game should be built **code-first**, not as a Unity project.

The recommended technical direction is a web-first multiplayer game using lightweight libraries:

- **TypeScript** as the main language.
- **PixiJS** for rendering if maximum control is desired.
- **Phaser** if faster prototyping is more important.
- **Colyseus** for multiplayer rooms and real-time synchronization.
- **Node.js** for the backend.
- **Postgres** for persistent data.
- **Docker** for deployment.
- **Aseprite** for sprites and animations.
- **LDtk** or **Tiled** for map creation.
- **Vite** for the frontend setup.

The project should not be built from raw WebGL or raw sockets from scratch. The goal is **code-first development with useful libraries**, not unnecessary engine-building.

## Recommended Architecture

The main overworld should **not** be modelled as disconnected room hops.

Recommended structure:

- `WorldMap`
  - the seamless player-facing overworld
- `WorldRegion`
  - authored region slices of the overworld
- `WorldChunk` / `WorldCell`
  - runtime materialization and sparse changed-state units
- `PersonalIslandInstance`
  - player-owned island space
- `DungeonInstance`
  - solo or group combat spaces
- `InteriorInstance`
  - optional private or special-purpose interiors

Visible map transitions are acceptable as prototype scaffolding or special-case travel, but they are not the final model for moving around the main overworld.

The server should be authoritative for important gameplay decisions such as movement validation, combat results, inventories, crafting, markets, island saves and worldstate changes.

The client should focus on rendering, input, UI, animation, effects and displaying synced multiplayer state.

## Data-Driven Content

The game should be built in a data-driven way so new content can be added without rewriting core systems.

Content that should be data-driven:

- Items
- Recipes
- Buildings
- Decorations
- Resources
- Skills
- Weapons
- Enemies
- Quests
- Events
- Worldstate values
- Island types

This supports the evergreen progression model because new islands, furniture sets, recipes, enemies, quests and resources can be added sideways over time without raising level caps or invalidating older content.

## Development Priority

For the first version, the technical goal is not to build a huge MMO.

The first goal is to prove that these things feel good:

- Walking around an isometric world.
- Seeing other players in a shared harbour town.
- Editing and personalizing a personal island.
- Gathering and crafting one useful item.
- Fighting one slow, readable enemy encounter.
- Moving between personal island, shared town and wild island.

The core technical prototype should prove the feeling of:

**my island → shared harbour → wild island → market → home**

## 21. Tone

The game is cozy-dark.

Not grimdark.
Not childish.
Not pure farming sim.
Not pure combat MMO.

Tone examples:

- Warm lanterns on a rainy dock.
- A personal garden beside a cursed ocean.
- Friendly markets with strange goods.
- Quiet ruins with old god bones.
- Cozy kitchens preparing food for dangerous expeditions.
- Small islands full of personality.
- Dangerous forests that still look beautiful.

## 22. First MVP

The first MVP should prove the core promise:

> Three independent paths with synergy inside a shared island world.

Do not build the full MMO first.

MVP content:

- One personal island system
- One shared harbour town
- One shared resource island
- One small ruin or dungeon
- One market board
- One worldstate variable
- One public event
- Three basic skills
- Three weapon styles
- One crafting chain
- One island upgrade chain

Recommended MVP paths:

### Builder MVP

- Player can customize island layout.
- Player can place buildings and decorations.
- Player can grow crops or create a functional island module.
- Player can progress without combat.

### Explorer MVP

- Player can travel to a shared island.
- Player can fight slow, small-scale encounters.
- Player can discover rare resources or lore.
- Player can sell loot to others.

### Crafter MVP

- Player can buy or gather materials.
- Player can craft useful goods.
- Player can sell goods through the market.
- Player can progress without combat.

MVP worldstate example:

- Lungwood Isle has a corruption value.
- If players ignore corruption, stronger enemies appear and certain resources become rarer.
- If players reduce corruption, safer routes open and new resources/quests appear.

## 23. Non-Goals

Do not build these early:

- One giant always-loaded overworld object kept fully active in memory
- Huge raid battles
- Full PvP economy warfare
- 50 skills
- 20 biomes
- Complex political systems
- Giant guild territory conquest
- Full AI-generated quest system
- WoW-style expansion treadmill
- Hack-and-slash combat
- Heavy survival punishment
- Mandatory base-building
- Mandatory crafting
- Mandatory combat progression

These can be considered later only if the core loop works.

## 24. Recommended Tech Direction

The game can be built without Unity.

Recommended code-first web stack:

- TypeScript
- PixiJS or Phaser for rendering/game client
- Colyseus for multiplayer rooms
- Node.js backend
- Postgres database
- Redis later if needed
- Tiled or LDtk for map editing
- Aseprite for pixel art
- Docker for deployment

The game should not be built from raw WebGL and sockets unless there is a very specific reason.

Use lightweight libraries, but keep the architecture code-first.

Recommended runtime structure:

- WorldMap / WorldRegion / WorldChunk
- PersonalIslandInstance
- DungeonInstance
- InteriorInstance
- MarketService
- WorldstateService
- AccountService
- InventoryService

## 25. Short Pitch

**Ashenwake Isles** is a cozy-dark isometric island MMO where players own fully customizable personal islands, specialize in building, exploring, or crafting, and participate in a shared archipelago shaped by player actions and evolving worldstates. The game avoids forced playstyles and WoW-like power resets. Instead, it uses OSRS-style evergreen progression, player-driven economy, deliberate small-scale combat, and dynamic island stories to create a living world where every player can choose their own role.

## 26. Design Mantra

> My island gives me identity.  
> The town gives me community.  
> The world gives me mystery.  
> The market connects us.  
> The worldstate makes it alive.
