## 2. STORY LOGIC

### 2.1 "Empty" raised, held, and abandoned — no thread movement across the chapter *(CRITICAL)*
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector), Role 4 (Writing Professional), Role 6 (Story Analyst)
- **Issue:** The chapter opens on the Trait mystery, holds it for three paragraphs, then shelves it entirely in favour of the creature fight. By the close, "Empty" has not advanced, complicated, or been actively deferred — it has simply been set aside. A cliffhanger that ended Chapter 2 and anchors Chapter 3's first scene cannot vanish without at least one beat of active acknowledgement at the chapter's close.
- **Fix:** Before the system notification or in Adrian's stillness after the third kill, add one line of Trait-awareness — not resolution, not new information, just active deferral: *He would check the Trait again once his hands had stopped shaking.* Then do not have him check it. The unresolved intention carries the thread without resolving it.
- [ ]

### 2.2 Runeheart pulse during first kill: no interior registration *(CRITICAL)*
- **Source:** Role 6 (Story Analyst)
- **Quote:** *"A hard pulse struck under his sternum and spread inward, hot and wrong, not through the skin but through the new structure that had taken root there. It felt like heated grit driving into the centre of his chest and staying there. His breath locked."*
- **Issue:** Adrian has had exactly one prior Runeheart experience. Here, the pulse locks his breath and drives heat through a new internal structure — and he rolls the dead weight off and continues fighting with zero interior notation. He gives more cognitive attention to the smell of the room and the texture of the creatures' rib membranes than to an anomalous event inside his own chest. The deferral of analysis is correct; the complete absence of registration is not consistent with his established cataloguing habit.
- **Fix:** One thought during or immediately after the second creature's attack — not analysis, just acknowledgment that something happened and requires later attention. It does not need to interrupt the fight's pace.
- [ ]

### 2.3 Maintenance passage entry carries no risk calculation
- **Source:** Role 6 (Story Analyst)
- **Quote:** *"He checked the arch again before he went in. The blue-white lines at the base had thickened into something closer to current. That was not comforting."*
- **Issue:** Adrian's defining professional habit is reading physical environments for failure modes before committing to them. The maintenance passage behind an actively energising arch is the one space in this chapter where that habit is entirely absent — he notes the arch is accelerating, and enters without any acknowledgment of the confinement risk. The decision to enter is plausible; the absence of the calculation that should precede it contradicts every prior movement through an unfamiliar space.
- **Fix:** One line where he clocks the confinement risk and decides it is acceptable. The decision itself can remain; it just needs to show the calculation.
- [ ]

## 3. STRUCTURAL

### 3.1 The chapter has two beginnings and no middle
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Issue:** Chapter 3 runs: [Empty investigation → exploration → creature fight → level-up]. The first two segments are both setup. Neither creates a complication that the fight then resolves. The Runeheart pulses during the fight are a step toward connecting the Empty thread with the action, but they are a reward mechanic rather than a complication. The chapter is three sequential events, not a structured scene. If the creature fight is the chapter's engine, it needs to be set up by something more than spatial discovery — ideally a condition created by the Empty trait, the arch's activation, or the dead stranger's presence that makes the fight harder or more costly.
- **Note:** A chapter-level structural flag, not a single fixable line. Carry forward as a drafting consideration for revision.
- [ ]

### 3.2 The arch is perpetually imminent and never arrives
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Issue:** The arch hummed at Chapter 2's close. It hums through Chapter 3's exploration. It deepens at Chapter 3's end. This is the third consecutive chapter beat where the arch registers as significant and does nothing. "Imminent threat" requires escalation or arrival. What exists now is a held note losing tension with each iteration.
- **Note:** A forward-looking flag. If the arch is not going to act in Chapter 4's opening, it needs to do something in Chapter 3 beyond deepen.
- [ ]

### 3.3 Opening Empty-examination runs longer than it earns
- **Source:** Role 4 (Writing Professional), Role 1 (Lector)
- **Quote:** *"Trait had been listed beside Class rather than beneath it, which suggested a second layer rather than a subclass, something separate that was meant to shape the first result."*
- **Issue:** The analytical paragraph that follows the two-line system dump has Adrian reasoning toward a conclusion the reader already drew at the end of Chapter 2. The chapter is summarising prior-chapter inference rather than generating new information. That is forward friction in the opening pages. The system-inspection register was also fully visited in Chapter 2; the chapter returns to it without adding new dimension.
- **Fix:** Compress the analytical paragraph. The core inference (seeded Trait is probably standard, he is the anomaly) is already stated in the preceding paragraph. What follows can be cut to one sentence covering the structural position insight (beside rather than beneath = second layer), landing faster on "he had been handed something bad."
- [ ]

### 3.4 Aftermath does not fully widen after sustained combat
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor), Role 1 (Lector)
- **Issue:** The widening passage ("He lowered it to the floor more carefully...") does real work but is a single paragraph before the chapter re-compresses into the level notification and close. For a combat sequence spanning roughly a third of the chapter, one widening paragraph is thin. The post-combat creature examination should be a second pass — staying with the physical and sensory detail longer before the system notification arrives.
- **Fix:** Expand the post-combat section. The creature observation paragraph should be extended by two to three sentences, staying inside the sensory aftermath rather than pivoting immediately to the level screen.
- [ ]

### 3.5 Relational investment axis absent at three chapters — no longer excusable
- **Source:** Role 4 (Writing Professional)
- **Issue:** Adrian has now completed three chapters as an entirely solitary protagonist. Chapter 1's relationship investment — the mother, the deliberately under-played grief — cannot carry relational weight across an indefinite solo origin arc. At three chapters, this is no longer a structural concession; it is a pattern. The genre contract requires relational investment to run in parallel with power progression from the first act.
- **Note:** A forward-looking flag for Chapter 4 or sooner. A voice through a wall. A silhouette. A name scratched somewhere. Something that gives the reader a direction that is not merely forward into more empty architecture.
- [ ]

## 4. AI TELLS + STYLE

  7. *"They were not boot prints."* — earns its isolation; strong candidate for second kept beat

### 4.3 Three-kill Runeheart: mechanically mirrored structure
- **Source:** Role 3 (AI Detector)
- **Issue:** Each kill closes on a Runeheart response in the same positional slot. The escalating variation is deliberate, but the three-beat pattern is structurally mirrored in a way that reads as generated rather than discovered.
- **Fix:** Deny the second kill its closing Runeheart beat entirely — let the absence make the third pulse land harder. The second pulse is the weakest of the three and can be cut or compressed to a brief physical sensation rather than a dedicated landing moment.
- [ ]

### 4.4 Self-correction move absent from both analytical passages
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Issue:** The Trait analysis section (four paragraphs) contains no self-correction. The body forensics section (two paragraphs) also contains none. The only self-correction in the chapter appears in combat ("So not sight, then. Vibration, smell, or something worse."). PROSE_ANALYSIS.md defines the self-correction move as a primary marker of the analytical voice. Its complete absence from the two analytical passages is a recurring flag across all three chapters.
- **Fix:** In the Trait analysis, after "he was the anomaly sitting where the explanation should have been," let Adrian initially read Empty as a sub-class modifier, then catch himself when he reconsiders the layout position ("Trait had been listed beside Class rather than beneath it") and revise. This also creates the pivot-word opener opportunity (see 4.5).
- [ ]

### 4.5 Pivot-word openers absent from analytical passages
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Issue:** Trait analysis: five paragraphs, no pivot opener. Body forensics: three consecutive paragraphs, no pivot opener. Same flag as Chapters 1 and 2. Pivot openers are the connective tissue of the analytical voice.
- **Fix:** Add at least one pivot opener in the Trait analysis ("Granted, the system only omitted definitions when it assumed the term was standard..." or "Admittedly, a seeded Trait was probably normal...") and one in the body forensics ("Granted, no obvious killing wound..."). Partially overlaps with 4.4 — the self-correction move naturally opens with a pivot word.
- [ ]

### 4.6 Casual intensifier drought
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Issue:** One instance in the chapter ("frankly, a bit ambitious" — well-placed). No "damn," "bloody," "hell," "pretty," "honestly," or "really" in ~3,800 words elsewhere. The injury acknowledgment and the Trait-analysis irritation are natural locations.
- **Fix:** Add two casual intensifiers. "Pain flared sharp and immediate" → "Pain flared sharp and bloody immediate" or similar. The Trait opening's irritation at the system is the other candidate.
- [ ]

### 4.7 Audible monologue below threshold for chapter length
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Issue:** Two spoken lines in the entire chapter: "Good. Something else is missing." and "Right. Excellent timing." Both are well-placed. Two lines is the floor, not "a few," for a chapter covering system analysis, exploration, corpse discovery, equipping, threat tracking, and a three-creature combat. The protagonist is audibly silent from line 34 to the chapter's end.
- **Fix:** Add one spoken line at or near the body discovery (Adrian's professional assessment of a stranger's death rendered entirely in internal narration loses the pressure-valve beat a spoken reaction provides), and one line post-combat.
- [ ]

### 4.8 One paragraph of generic register
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Quote:** *"Whoever had worked back here had been keeping a difficult system barely functional and doing it with whatever survived the argument."*
- **Issue:** The preceding sentence is character-filtered and strong. This sentence is atmospheric summary — thematic conclusion rather than technical inference. Any competent narrator could write it. Compare to the hatch analysis ("fasteners at the corners had been turned often enough to break the rust pattern") which is specific to Adrian's professional reading. The maintenance-bay passage should deliver the same level of specificity: what do the patch-plate materials, the bracket mismatches, the weld-over-weld pattern tell him about failure mode and frequency?
- **Fix:** Replace with something Adrian would actually infer from the physical evidence. The materials, the bracket mismatches, or the weld pattern should tell him something specific — even if what it tells him is uncertain.
- [ ]

## 5. LINE FIXES

### 5.1 "That was not comforting" redundant with "Excellent timing"
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector), Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Quote:** *"That was not comforting. / 'Right,' he muttered. 'Excellent timing.'"*
- **Fix:** Cut "That was not comforting." The sardonic spoken line carries both beats.
- [ ]

### 5.2 "Lodged in his skull like a splinter" — soft-focus simile inconsistent with register
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Quote:** *"...the arch in mind instead of the word still lodged in his skull like a splinter."*
- **Issue:** The simile is the chapter's first literary-soft moment in an otherwise forensically dry prose register. Also contradicts the "dismissed" action immediately preceding it.
- **Fix:** Cut the simile. "...started round the chamber again, the arch drawing his attention before his feet had finished deciding to move."
- [ ]

### 5.3 Arch inspection paragraph: four flat observation sentences
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Quote:** *"The frame had not been built for beauty. That much was obvious. It had been built to survive load, heat, vibration, and the kind of repetitive maintenance you only expected when shutting a thing down was either impossible or catastrophically expensive. Cable trunks disappeared into the surrounding walls in grouped runs instead of neat hidden feeds. Inspection hatches sat at regular intervals around the inner frame, plain and practical. Somebody had expected known failures and built access around them."*
- **Fix:** "The frame had not been built for beauty. Cable trunks ran in grouped bundles to the walls, inspection hatches at regular intervals, no pretence at concealment. Somebody had designed the thing expecting known failures and built access around every one of them."
- [ ]

### 5.4 Lamp dump — "dragged" detail buried in undifferentiated fragments
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Quote:** *"The room sharpened at once. Scrape marks on the bench. Repeated tool scoring near a recessed floor panel. Thin dark smears near the viewing slit, dragged rather than splashed."*
- **Issue:** The smear detail is the dangerous observation; it receives the same format as the others.
- **Fix:** "The room sharpened at once. Scrape marks on the bench. Repeated tool scoring near a recessed floor panel. Near the viewing slit, dark smears had been dragged across the wall — not splashed, dragged, which was a different problem entirely."
- [ ]

### 5.5 "It took longer than he wanted" — redundant qualifier before physical cost
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector), Role 3 (AI Detector)
- **Quote:** *"It took longer than he wanted, long enough for both shoulders to start shaking under the strain."*
- **Fix:** "Long enough for both shoulders to start shaking under the strain."
- [ ]

### 5.6 Knife fumble syntactically buried at highest-danger moment
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector)
- **Quote:** *"He got one forearm up under the creature's jaw and dragged the blade free with his other hand by feel. The knife caught on the sheath lip on the way out and nearly slipped."*
- **Fix:** "He got one forearm up under the creature's jaw and dragged the blade free with his other hand by feel. The sheath lip caught it. For a half-second it was going nowhere. Then his grip found the handle and pulled."
- [ ]

### 5.7 "Some other design that had given up halfway" — authorial frame Adrian cannot have
- **Source:** Role 1 (Lector), Role 3 (AI Detector)
- **Quote:** *"The rib membranes had a stretched, unfinished quality to them, as if they belonged to some other design that had given up halfway."*
- **Issue:** A manufacturing narrative that Adrian has no information to support.
- **Fix:** "The rib membranes had a stretched, unfinished quality, as if the frame inside them had been built for something larger and never filled out." Stays within observable anatomy.
- [ ]

### 5.8 "Warmth was warmth" — cliché pairing
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Quote:** *"...warmth was warmth and he was in no position to be choosy."*
- **Fix:** Replace with something less formulaic that stays in Adrian's register. The function (accepting suboptimal gear) can remain; the phrasing should not be a common-language cliché.
- [ ]

### 5.9 "Hurt was still hurt here" — states what action already demonstrated
- **Source:** Role 3 (AI Detector)
- **Quote:** *"Hurt was still hurt here."*
- **Issue:** The observation that injuring the creature changes its behaviour is already demonstrated: "The first one, the one he had punched through the chest earlier, was moving more carefully now. Dark fluid ran down one foreleg." The sentence translates the demonstrated fact into a statement.
- **Fix:** Cut it. The behaviour is the proof.
- [ ]

### 5.10 "Useful to know. Late, but useful." — over-composed self-assessment; too slow
- **Source:** Role 3 (AI Detector), Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Quote:** *"So it really was tracking vibration. / Useful to know. Late, but useful."*
- **Issue:** "So it really was tracking vibration" is confirmation the reader already has. "Late, but useful" labels the irony rather than letting it sit.
- **Fix:** Cut "So it really was tracking vibration." Keep "Useful to know." Cut "Late, but useful."
- [ ]

### 5.11 Rule of three: "injury, exhaustion, and a problem" — third item does all the work
- **Source:** Role 3 (AI Detector), Role 5 (Style Auditor)

### 5.15 "A second shape came through the seam behind it" — "behind it" ambiguous
- **Source:** Role 2 (Royal Road Reader)
- **Quote:** *"A second shape came through the seam behind it."*

### 5.16 Body description: three subject-verb-complement declaratives in parallel
- **Source:** Role 5 (Style Auditor)
- **Quote:** *"Cloth had gone stiff and grimy. Leather straps still crossed what had once been a chest. The skull tilted into the corner."*
- **Issue:** Three parallel short declaratives, identical skeleton, describing one static scene.
- **Fix:** Replace one declarative with an inference or comparison that carries Adrian's professional reading: the materials, what their condition implies about how long the body has been here relative to the ration wrappers.
- [ ]

### 5.17 "Something being claimed rather than merely received" — thematic declaration at mechanical moment
- **Source:** Role 3 (AI Detector)
- **Quote:** *"Heat moved through the centre of him and sank into the Runeheart with the slow certainty of something being claimed rather than merely received."*
- **Issue:** Converts a mechanical event into a thematic statement about agency. The chapter would be more unsettling if the pulse were just heat with no interpretive frame attached.
- **Fix:** Cut "with the slow certainty of something being claimed rather than merely received." End at "sank into the Runeheart."
- **Note:** This conflicts somewhat with keeping the differentiation between the three pulses (see 4.3). If the third pulse's distinctive quality is removed, the mechanical distinction should be preserved some other way — perhaps through physical sensation alone rather than meaning-making language.

## 6. ENGAGEMENT

### 6.1 Laminated scrap and unfamiliar civilisation — wanted one beat of recognition
- **Source:** Role 2 (Royal Road Reader)
- **Quote:** *"A laminated scrap with more writing on it, stiff enough to survive and useless enough to keep for later."*
- **Issue:** He is holding an object from the nearest person he has encountered since waking. The script on the wrappers, the token, the scrap — he is standing in the remnants of a civilisation that exists nowhere in his frame of reference. The chapter treats this as logistics. One short beat (even a single dry line) acknowledging the scope of unfamiliarity would give the world-building gesture more weight without slowing the pacing.
- **Note:** A gentle forward-looking suggestion; not a hard requirement.
- [ ]

### 6.2 First creature — hold the "wrong" quality a beat longer before launch
- **Source:** Role 2 (Royal Road Reader)
- **Quote:** *"It paused half clear of the wall and worked its jaw at the air."*
- **Issue:** The jaw working at the air is the creepiest detail in the description. The reader wanted it held a beat longer before the creature committed. The description is clinical, which is correct for Adrian; the single atmospheric beat could have more weight.
- **Note:** A light engagement note; easily addressed in a revision pass.

## 7. THE WRITING PROFESSIONAL'S QUESTION
