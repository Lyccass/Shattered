## CATEGORY 1: STRUCTURAL TELL THRESHOLDS (AGENTS.md)

### FLAG 1.1 — One-sentence paragraph landing beats (over threshold)

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** One-word paragraph landing beats — max 2 per chapter. More broadly, isolated single-sentence paragraphs functioning as compression beats are subject to the same logic: their force is proportional to their rarity.

The chapter uses the following isolated single-sentence paragraphs as deliberate landing punches outside active combat or mid-fight action beats:

1. **"The system declined to improve."** — Standalone. Follows the first dialogue line (wry echo). Lands cleanly. One of the two strongest.
2. **"That was not comforting."** — Standalone. Follows the description of the arch current thickening.
3. **"He ducked through the hatch."** — Standalone. A transition beat, not a punch, but formatted as one.
4. **"More helpful than most of the system so far."** — Standalone. Follows the system's item notification.
5. **"He went to the rack first."** — Standalone. A movement beat given paragraph isolation it does not need.
6. **"Adrian stopped and looked properly."** — Standalone. Follows the hand discovery. Earns its isolation.
7. **"They were not boot prints."** — Standalone. Clear threat-reveal beat. Earns its isolation.

That is seven instances outside the active combat exchange. The rule permits two. Items 1 and 6 or 1 and 7 are the strongest candidates for preservation. Items 3, 4, and 5 are transition or minor-observation beats that should be absorbed into surrounding text.

**Correction for Item 3:** Absorb into the previous paragraph ending: "...the blue-white lines at the base had thickened into something closer to current, which was not comforting. He ducked through the hatch." Saves a paragraph; preserves the wry note.

**Correction for Items 4 and 5:** Item 4 can close the item-notification paragraph as a clause. Item 5 can open the tool-description paragraph rather than standing alone.

### FLAG 1.2 — Two-line contrast or pivot stacks (over threshold)

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** Two-line contrast or pivot stacks — max 2 per chapter.

**Instance A:**
> "Empty could wait. The machine probably would not."

Two-sentence contrast paragraph. The second exists to invert the first. Earns its form; sharpest pivot in the chapter. Preserve.

**Instance B:**
> "Small, then."
> "And definitely more than one."

Split across two consecutive single-sentence paragraphs. Together: size assessment / count escalation. This is one sentence given artificial paragraph isolation. Should be merged: "Small, then. And definitely more than one." — the rhythm survives; the redundant split disappears.

**Instance C:**
> "So it really was tracking vibration."
> "Useful to know. Late, but useful."

Two stacking units — standalone inference / two-beat self-correction pair — performing a pivot stack. "Useful to know. Late, but useful." is itself an internal contrast pair. Three close punches in formation. Cut "So it really was tracking vibration." (the reader already knows; the confirmation is superfluous) and keep only "Useful to know. Late, but useful." as the sole landing.

Three identifiable two-line contrast stacks. Rule flags at more than two. Instance A preserved; Instances B and C both need adjustment.

### FLAG 1.4 — Parallel stacking / tri-line blocks (approaching threshold)

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** More than three clearly similar vertical stacks in one chapter is a revision target.

**Instance A — body description:**
> "Cloth had gone stiff and grimy. Leather straps still crossed what had once been a chest. The skull tilted into the corner."

Three parallel short declaratives, all subject-verb-complement, all describing one static body. Audible skeleton match across all three.

**Instance B — damage inventory before final kill:**
> "Warmth ran down his left side from the ribs. The cuts across his collarbone burned when he tightened his grip. The whole bay hummed around him."

Three parallel sentences: physical sensation / physical sensation / ambient sensation. The third breaks the body-damage pattern, which partially saves it, but the tri-sentence shape is identical to Instance A.

Two clear vertical stacks. Not yet at the "more than three" threshold, but the pattern is present. Instance A is the revision priority: one of the three declaratives should be replaced with an inference or comparison. Adrian is a man who reads age and damage off surfaces — his body-examination should carry a deductive beat.

### FLAG 1.5 — Rule of three (default completeness lists)

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** Lists should have the number of items the thought actually requires.

**Instance A:** *"injury, exhaustion, and a problem that had outlasted the person trying to fix it"* — three-part cause-of-death summary. The first two are a natural pair. The third does the actual work.

**Fix:** "Injury and exhaustion, and something that had outlasted them both."

**Instance B:** *"oil, dust, and the stale mineral stink of closed places"* — three sensory items. The third carries the payload. "Oil and the stale mineral stink" reaches the distinctive detail faster.

Both minor in isolation; together they reinforce a default-to-three habit. Instance A is the revision priority.

## CATEGORY 2: ANTI-AI TELL LOCK (AGENTS.md)

### FLAG 2.1 — Polished landing lines at high frequency

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** Do not keep writing polished emotional landing lines just because they sound good. One or two sharp landings are craft. A steady stream is a tell.

Beyond the isolated-paragraph beats in Flag 1.1, the chapter produces polished closing formulations embedded in longer paragraphs:

- *"...which, granted, was how most weapons started."* (closes tool identification)
- *"...warmth was warmth and he was in no position to be choosy."* (closes the clothing-up sequence)
- *"Blood from the dead ones had changed the air. So had his."* (closes pre-final-creature pause)
- *"The thing fought for several seconds after the outcome had become obvious, which he resented on principle."* (closes the pinning sequence)

Each individually well-executed. Collectively: the chapter does not have passages that simply arrive where the observation ends. Nearly every unit closes on something quotable.

Strongest revision candidate: "warmth was warmth and he was in no position to be choosy" — "warmth was warmth" is a cliché pairing and the second clause restates what the first implies. Revise to something less formulaic. "Blood from the dead ones had changed the air. So had his." — sharpest line in the sequence, keep it.

---

### FLAG 2.2 — Aftermath does not fully widen

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** After pressure scenes, widen back out. The aftermath should breathe differently from the peak tension.

The widening passage ("He lowered it to the floor more carefully...") is five sentences and holds an observational register. It does the required work. However it is a single paragraph before the chapter re-compresses into the system notification and close. For a combat that ran across roughly a third of the chapter's length, one widening paragraph is thin. The post-combat creature examination should be a second pass, staying with the physical and sensory detail longer before the system notification arrives.

---

### FLAG 2.3 — One paragraph of generic register

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** The narration must sound like one particular mind.

> "Whoever had worked back here had been keeping a difficult system barely functional and doing it with whatever survived the argument."

The preceding sentence ("The passage behind the ring curved tight round the back of the arch and looked exactly like what happened when several generations of maintenance crews inherited the same impossible machine and slightly worse budgets") is character-filtered and strong. This sentence is atmospheric summary — thematic conclusion rather than technical inference. Any competent narrator could write it. Compare to the hatch analysis ("Its rim held a cleaner line than the surrounding frame, and the fasteners at the corners had been turned often enough to break the rust pattern") — that is specific to Adrian's professional reading of surfaces. The maintenance-bay passage should deliver the same: what do the patch-plate materials, the bracket mismatches, the weld-over-weld pattern tell him about how often this system failed and in what mode?

## CATEGORY 3: SENTENCE RHYTHM AND PARAGRAPH SHAPE (PROSE_ANALYSIS.md)

### FLAG 3.1 — Missing analytical sentence in the body forensics

**Rule (PROSE_ANALYSIS.md):** Rhythm: two or three medium sentences → longer sentence for reasoning → short sentence to land → repeat.

> "There was no obvious killing wound and no major break suggesting a fight. The stained bandage and the posture of the body suggested something meaner and simpler: injury, exhaustion, and a problem that had outlasted the person trying to fix it."

Two medium sentences delivering the conclusion, with no longer analytical sentence building the inferential chain before the payoff. The reasoning skips its middle step: Adrian moves from "no obvious wound" directly to "meaner and simpler" without showing what the bandage placement indicated, what the body posture pattern matched, or what combination of clues drove the injury/exhaustion conclusion. One longer analytical sentence between the observation and the summary would correct the rhythm and add the inferential texture the character's professional background warrants.

The first Runeheart pulse is four sentences — appropriate. The next beat is a single-sentence paragraph ("The second creature was still alive.") — justified because the creature is already moving. Minor notation only; the pattern is consistent with combat context.

## CATEGORY 4: DICTION AND VOICE PATTERNS (AGENTS.md / PROSE_ANALYSIS.md)

### FLAG 4.1 — Self-correction move absent from analytical passages

**Rule (PROSE_ANALYSIS.md):** The self-correction move must appear in analytical voice. Its absence from extended reasoning passages is a flag.

The Trait analysis section (four paragraphs) contains no self-correction. Adrian works through the reading in a single uninterrupted chain: system behavior → what a seeded Trait probably is → why its absence is bad. He does not catch himself, revise a claim, or undercut a preliminary interpretation.

The body forensics section (two paragraphs) also contains no self-correction.

The single self-correction in the chapter appears in combat: "So not sight, then. Vibration, smell, or something worse." — correctly placed, but inside a combat beat, not a reasoning passage.

**Correction:** In the Trait analysis, after "he was the anomaly sitting where the explanation should have been," Adrian could initially read Empty as a sub-class modifier, then catch himself when he reconsiders the layout position ("Trait had been listed beside Class rather than beneath it") and revise. This spreads the analytical reasoning and adds the inferential texture the style requires.

---

### FLAG 4.2 — Casual intensifier drought in narration

**Rule (AGENTS.md / PROSE_ANALYSIS.md):** Casual intensifiers (pretty, damn, hell, bloody, honestly, really, kind of) are part of the voice.

The chapter contains one instance: "movement seemed, frankly, a bit ambitious" — well-placed. No "damn," "bloody," "hell," "pretty," "honestly," or "really" elsewhere in narration or dialogue across approximately 3,800 words.

Improvement over Chapter 2 (zero instances), but still thin for a chapter this long with combat and an injury assessment. "Pain flared sharp and immediate" registers clinical. The injury acknowledgment is a natural location for "bloody" or "damn." The Trait-analysis opening (Adrian's irritation at the system) is another.

## CATEGORY 5: DIALOGUE LOCK (AGENTS.md)

### FLAG 5.1 — Audible monologue below threshold for chapter length

**Rule (AGENTS.md):** A solo chapter should include at least a few lines of audible monologue.

The chapter contains two spoken lines: *"Good. Something else is missing."* and *"Right. Excellent timing."* Both are well-placed. Two lines is the minimum floor, not "a few," for a chapter covering system analysis, exploration, locker discovery, a corpse, equipping, threat tracking, and a three-creature combat. The protagonist is audibly silent from approximately line 34 to the chapter's end.

The body discovery and the post-combat aftermath are both natural moments for a dry muttered comment. The body particularly: Adrian's professional assessment of a stranger's death is rendered entirely in internal narration, losing the pressure-valve beat a spoken reaction provides.

**Correction:** Add one line at or near the body discovery, one line post-combat. Neither needs to be more than a single dry sentence.

## CATEGORY 6: OPENER ROTATION (PROSE_ANALYSIS.md)

### FLAG 6.1 — Pivot-word openers absent from analytical and forensics passages

**Rule (PROSE_ANALYSIS.md):** Pivot words should not be absent for more than four consecutive paragraphs of reasoning.

The Trait analysis section: five paragraphs of analytical reasoning, openers in sequence — "He focused on it" / "He pushed again" / "Trait had been listed" / "He did not need much more." No pivot opener across five paragraphs.

The body forensics section: three consecutive reasoning paragraphs, no pivot openers.

This is the same flag noted in Chapters 1 and 2. Pivot openers are the connective tissue of the analytical voice; their sustained absence produces narration that sounds assembled rather than reasoned.

**Correction:** Add at least one pivot opener in the Trait analysis ("Granted, the system only omitted definitions when it assumed the term was standard..." or "Admittedly, a seeded Trait was probably normal...") and one in the body forensics ("Granted, no obvious killing wound..."). The correction partially overlaps with the self-correction fix (Flag 4.1), since pivot openers are the natural entry point for revision moves in this style.

## OVERALL COMPLIANCE SCORE: 6.5 / 10

Chapter 3 improves on Chapter 2 across several meaningful dimensions. The combat is spatially legible and follows the Combat Lock cleanly — every exchange answers what was tried, why, and what it cost. The protagonist moves on instinct before analysis throughout. The hard bans are all clear: no omniscient narration, no POV drift, no purple prose, no vague action writing. The chapter ends on forward pressure. The Runeheart mechanic is integrated without system-text overload. The chapter's best line ("movement seemed, frankly, a bit ambitious") is exactly the right register.

The dominant failure cluster is, as in Chapter 2, **landing-line saturation combined with isolated single-sentence paragraphs used as compression beats outside combat**, at a frequency that trains the reader to anticipate the unit's closing punch rather than feel it. Seven standalone paragraphs outside active combat (Flag 1.1), three two-line contrast stacks (Flag 1.2), and a steady supply of polished closing formulations embedded in longer paragraphs (Flag 2.1) together produce a chapter that sounds shaped from the outside — each unit driven toward its conclusion — rather than discovered from inside the protagonist's experience. The self-correction move remains absent from both analytical passages (Flag 4.1), compounding the impression of composed narration. The two-line monologue budget is thin for a chapter of this length and event density (Flag 5.1). Priority revisions: collapse the five extraneous landing-beat paragraphs (keeping the two strongest), insert one self-correction in the Trait analysis, add at least two audible monologue lines across the chapter's latter two-thirds, and add one or two pivot-word sentence openers in the analytical passages.
