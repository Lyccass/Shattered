# Royal Road Reader — Chapter 5 Review

## First Impression

Okay, I will be honest. The opening got me. "Darkness pressed in from every side so completely that even his own mind felt briefly distant." That is actually a good line. I was ready to keep reading.

Then it gave me: "Then the world came back all at once."

And then — grass. Hills. Mountains. The horizon. An arch standing by itself.

Fine. I have read this kind of reveal a hundred times on Royal Road and it is fine. The beats are correct. Adrian checks if he can go back, he cannot, he moves. The internal voice is dry and British and I like it: *"This is crazy. Fine."* That is him. I recognise him from chapter one and I am happy to spend time with this version of a person.

The first three paragraphs did their job. I was in.

---

## The Walk Up the Hill

This is where I slowed down and started reading more carefully, which is actually a compliment. Adrian noticing he is not some fantasy superhero, that his ribs still hurt and his knee is still bad — I bought that completely. After two chapters of him fighting things and barely surviving, it would have felt like a cheat if he suddenly bounded up hillsides. I liked "his new stats had not turned him into some untouchable fantasy hero. They had just made him more capable than he had been." That is a good line. That is the kind of thing that makes me trust a writer to not suddenly give the protagonist god mode.

One line broke my flow: "drank sparingly some of the water ressources he had found in the place he had waken up in." That is a clumsy sentence. "Water resources" sounds like a government report. And "the place he had waken up in" instead of "the facility" or even just "back there" — it reads like a placeholder that never got fixed.

---

## The Path and the Marker Post

I liked this stretch. I liked that finding a path meant finding people, and that finding people was not automatically good news. "Civilisation. Immediately complicated." Made me smile. This is the version of Adrian I want to follow.

The description of the writing on the marker post worked well. The bit about someone having refreshed part of the inscription after the stone had already stood a long while — that is the kind of small worldbuilding detail that makes me feel like there is something real behind the story. I leaned in there.

---

## Tracking the Group

This section I enjoyed the most. The paragraph describing how the group moved — wide enough apart that one problem would not catch all of them at once, tight enough nobody drifted out of help — told me immediately these were experienced people without anyone announcing it. No stat box. No NPC info dump. I just watched them cross a hillside and understood. That is good reading.

"The gear sat on them as if it belonged there." Yes. Exactly.

I held my breath during the moment where the man stopped and stared directly toward Adrian's patch of grass. I knew Adrian had not been seen, because there was still chapter left, but I believed it enough to feel the tension. The "thank fuck" moment when Adrian confirmed they were fully human was earned.

---

## Where I Slowed Down

The Keiran section dropped me out of the momentum completely.

I understand what it is doing. It introduces a second POV, shows Adrian was watched the whole time. Fine. But right when I was invested in Adrian's decision about whether to approach the group — it cuts to someone on a ridge watching him.

I did not want to be on the ridge. I wanted to be in the grass with Adrian.

The Keiran section is not bad writing. "What a curious little fella" is a good line. But the placement hurt the tension. I went from "is he going to be caught, what is he going to do" — to "here is an observer recapping what already happened." The suspense deflated. When the chapter cut back to Adrian, I had to rebuild my investment.

---

## The Boar Fight

That hit hard. I did not see it coming and neither did Adrian. "Something crashed through the grass, charging right at him" is a perfect sentence. The fight was chaotic and fast and hurt in the right places — his ribs, his knee, the spear getting ripped away. And then the knife went in under the jaw as a last desperate move.

And then the knife broke.

That ending. I said something unkind out loud. That is exactly what a chapter ending should do.

One small thing: "What is this thing?" Adrian cursed under his breath" — "cursed under his breath" usually means an expletive, not a question. It is a slightly odd construction that a non-native reader might find confusing.

---

## What Would Have Made Me Happier

One: the water resources sentence needs a real fix. It is the only line that reads genuinely unfinished.

Two: the Keiran POV break needed a proper hard section divider so I could adjust instead of being dropped in mid-momentum. Keep Keiran, but signal the shift clearly.

---

## Language Flags (typos and broken sentences)

- "ressources" — misspelled
- "beeing" — misspelled
- "bu it did tell him" — missing space/word
- "studipid" — misspelled
- "the I have the feeling it will not be in favour for me" — broken sentence structure, wrong apostrophe character

These are scattered enough to create background friction for any careful reader.

---

## Overall

This is the first chapter where I felt like the story was going somewhere real. The world outside Helix felt alive. The armed strangers felt like actual people. The boar fight earned its space and ended on exactly the right beat.

The Keiran break cost some momentum, and the typos need a pass. But these are fixable problems. The fundamentals are good. Adrian is still someone I want to follow.

**Would read the next one** — because the knife broke and I need to know if he dies in chapter six, and also because those strangers on the hillside are interesting and I want to see what happens when he has to actually talk to them.
