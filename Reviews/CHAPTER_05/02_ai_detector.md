## Structural Tells

### Rule of Three

**Hit 1**
> "Grass rolled out across low hills ahead of him, small clusters of trees broke the open ground at uneven intervals, and mountains stood far off against the horizon."

Three-item landscape inventory — hills, trees, mountains — structured as a complete visual survey. The third item is the most distanced and most cinematic, following the standard escalation from near to far, concrete to grand.

Suggested rewrite: End on the most specific or strangest detail rather than the grandest. "Small clusters of trees broke the open ground at uneven intervals. Mountains sat far off. Nothing else."

**Hit 2**
> "His ribs still pulled when he breathed too deeply, his knee had not forgiven the earlier fight, and the simple act of hauling himself uphill with a spear and a half-understood body proved quickly enough that his new stats had not turned him into some untouchable fantasy hero."

Three-item injury catalogue (ribs, knee, general exertion) structured to build from specific complaints to a thematic conclusion. The third item is the longest, most abstract, and carries the interpretive payload — the standard AI escalation pattern.

Suggested rewrite: "His ribs still pulled when he breathed too deeply, and his knee had not forgiven the earlier fight. Hauling himself uphill with a spear proved the stats had limits."

**Hit 3**
> "He adjusted his grip on the spear, picked the nearest rise with enough height to show him more of the land, and started walking."

Three-beat action triplet: grip, target, movement. Three actions that together constitute a full decision-and-execution cycle. Natural action prose would more often carry two steps and imply the third.

Suggested rewrite: "He adjusted his grip on the spear and started walking towards the nearest rise."

**Hit 4**
> "No local marks worth noticing, dead-Earth salvage worn with no understanding of how wrong it looked, fresh injuries, and a spear carried like a walking stick rather than a chosen discipline."

Four-item observation list in Keiran's POV. The final item ("a spear carried like a walking stick rather than a chosen discipline") is the longest, most analytical, and most characterful — escalating from raw data to interpretive conclusion. The phrase "chosen discipline" reaches for a register the other items don't inhabit, which is a generation tell.

Suggested rewrite: "A spear carried wrong — walking-stick grip, no discipline. Dead-Earth salvage, fresh injuries, and no local marks."

### Negative Parallelisms

**Hit 1**
> "There was no chamber behind it now, no dark corridor, no trace of the place he had stepped out of."

"No X, no Y, no Z" — three-beat negative enumeration moving from large to small (chamber → corridor → trace), giving the paragraph a rhetorical sense of completeness-through-negation.

Suggested rewrite: "The chamber was gone. The corridor, the darkness — all of it. The arch just stood there."

**Hit 2**
> "There was no reason to assume they spoke English. No reason to assume they could read anything he wrote, if he even had a way to write it. No reason to assume they would see a lone stranger carrying a spear and dressed in a coat stolen from a corpse and think anything charitable at all."

Classic triple-negative-parallelism cascade. Three discrete concerns in parallel, each escalating in concreteness (language → literacy → threat perception). The most overt instance of the pattern in the chapter.

Suggested rewrite: "No reason to assume they spoke English, and less reason to assume they'd look at a stranger with a stolen spear and react charitably."

**Hit 3**
> "Not as deep as he wanted but deep enough to matter."

Compressed "Not X but Y" negative parallelism used as a combat beat qualifier. Too neat and balanced for a man in a sliding fight.

Suggested rewrite: "Deep enough." Let the next sentence carry the consequence.

### Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis Reasoning

**Hit 1**
> "Alone was bad. Approaching blind might be worse. The trouble was that by the look of the country around him, dark would soon begin making both options uglier."

Three-sentence internal reasoning sequence: Position → Complication → Synthesis. Each sentence handles exactly one variable. The conclusion performs a precise dual-reference back to both prior sentences ("both options uglier").

Suggested rewrite: "Dark was going to make every option worse. That was the conclusion, anyway, and it didn't help."

Three consecutive sentences of near-equal length in Keiran's POV, each carrying one analytical observation. Assertion, endorsement, supporting generalisation — metronomic rhythm with no irregularity.

### Parenthetical Explanations Mid-Sentence

**Hit 1**
> "Their spacing was practical instead, wide enough that one problem would not catch all of them at once and tight enough that nobody drifted out of help."

The clause "wide enough that one problem would not catch all of them at once" translates physical spacing into its underlying tactical principle for the reader's benefit. A character observing from cover would register the effect, not articulate the reasoning behind it.

Suggested rewrite: "Their spacing was practical — not bunched, not scattered."

**Hit 2**
> "Its shoulders came nearly to his hip even with the angle of the slope against it, and its small eyes had that flat animal certainty that meant it had already decided what he was for."

"That meant it had already decided what he was for" glosses a physical detail (flat eyes) with an interpretation. The detail should carry the menace unaided.

Suggested rewrite: "Its shoulders came nearly to his hip, and its small eyes had the flat animal certainty of something that had already made up its mind."

### Over-Symmetrical Paragraph Endings

**Hit 1**
> "That did not tell Adrian who they were, but it did tell him the weapons were real."

Balanced antithetical conclusion ("not X, but Y") closing a paragraph of observation. Provides clean interpretive closure rather than leaving the observation open.

**Hit 2**
> "People, then. Current people, not just the cold remains of a dead facility and its dead worker."

One-sentence paragraph used as a thematic kicker. "Dead facility" and "dead worker" are a matched pair delivering a rhetorical close.

Suggested rewrite: End the section on the boot prints. "Boot prints had dried hard in the ground and survived long enough to keep their shape."

**Hit 3**
> "That was already more useful than the rest."

Paragraph-closing summary-evaluation sentence following a sequence of deductions about the boot prints. Performs the same function as "Logistics were armour" in Chapter 1 — a short declarative telling the reader the value of what was just observed.

Suggested rewrite: Cut it.

## Dialogue Tells

### Emotional Declarations Stated Directly Rather Than Implied

**Hit 1**
> "a tension he didnt even know he held left his body in an instant."

The emotional experience (unconscious anxiety releasing on positive discovery) is named directly. The character has just found signs of human habitation; the moment should be shown through body or behaviour, not labelled.

Suggested rewrite: "He straightened without meaning to."

**Hit 2**
> "Adrian had not realised quite how tightly some part of him had been wound around that question until it loosened."

Near-duplicate of Hit 1 in function, appearing later in the same chapter at the confirmation of human faces. The tension-release pattern has now been stated explicitly twice.

Suggested rewrite: Replace with a physical beat. "He let the breath out through his nose and kept looking."

**Hit 3**
> "The relief lasted right up until he heard them."

The word "relief" names the emotional state before the pivot that undercuts it. The sentence pre-interprets the reader's experience.

Suggested rewrite: "He had been glad for about four seconds."

### Agreement-then-Pivot Structure

**Hit 1**
> "Human is good. Human who cannot understand me is less good."

Two-line internal dialogue following a concession-pivot structure: affirmation → qualification. A miniature version of "You're right. However…" The format is too tidy for authentic thought under stress.

Suggested rewrite: Run them in one sentence or introduce something irregular between them.

### Characters Explaining Things for the Reader's Benefit

**Hit 1**
> "The man had the expression of someone trying very hard to remain functional while reality became progressively less cooperative."

Keiran's POV. The phrasing is a precise psychological précis delivered as quick read. A seasoned observer would register posture and movement; the narration delivers a fully formed characterisation instead.

Suggested rewrite: "The man moved like someone keeping very careful track of his own reactions — chin down, spear grip tight, scanning constantly."

## Narrative Red Flags

### Repeated Structural Mirroring Across Scenes

**Hit 1**

The tension-release pattern (unconscious anxiety relieved by confirmation of human normalcy) appears twice within the same chapter.

**Instance 1** (path and boot prints):
> "a tension he didnt even know he held left his body in an instant."

**Instance 2** (confirmation of human faces):
> "Adrian had not realised quite how tightly some part of him had been wound around that question until it loosened."

Two structurally identical beats using near-identical architecture — the discovery of something previously unconscious, expressed through the language of loosening or release. The second instance does not register as discovery because the reader has already received the pattern.

Suggested approach: Cut Instance 1. The face-confirmation scene is the stronger payoff; reserve the pattern for it.

### Protagonist Growth Signalled by Explicit Statement

**Hit 1**
> "They had just made him more capable than he had been."

The sentence explicitly states the lesson of the stats after the reader has already witnessed Adrian struggling uphill. The demonstration preceded the statement; the statement is redundant.

Suggested rewrite: Cut it.

### "Telling" Emotions with Clinical Precision

**Hit 1**
> "The voices drifted upslope in pieces when the wind turned, low at first and then clearer as the group slowed near another bend in the path. Human voices, human cadence, real conversation moving back and forth with the ordinary ease of people who expected to be understood."

"The ordinary ease of people who expected to be understood" abstracts a sensory experience into a social-psychological characterisation. This replaces what the voices sounded like with a statement about what their register implies.

Suggested rewrite: Stay in sensation. "The voices were easy with each other — no hesitation, no raised volume, the back-and-forth of people who'd been walking together long enough."
