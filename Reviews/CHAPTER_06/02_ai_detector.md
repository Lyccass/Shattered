## Structural Tells

### Negative Parallelisms

**Hit 1:**
> "Running was not viable. His knee had opinions about running. His ribs had louder ones."

Label: **Negative parallelism**. The structure establishes a thing, then escalates through denial in a controlled two-step. The escalation ("louder ones") is too neatly capped. A human writer in survival-panic mode would more likely tumble through this reasoning without the rhetorical finish.

Suggested rewrite: "Running was not viable — his knee had objections and his ribs had louder ones, and the slope would fix the rest."

---

**Hit 2:**
> "Not the kind that issued orders and expected silence in return, but the kind that had enough weight to make arguments short."

Label: **Negative parallelism**. The sentence is built in the classic AI pattern: define what a thing is not, then define what it is. This functions as a characterisation template rather than a natural observation.

Suggested rewrite: "The kind of authority that didn't need to raise its voice — it made arguments short, not absent."

---

### Mechanical Comparison Parallelism

**Hit 1:**
> "He spoke to the group as he descended, a few easy sentences, and whatever he said produced visible results. The arrow came down the rest of the way. The big man stepped back from where he had been flanking. The tension across the slope dropped to something that still contained wariness but had stopped feeling immediately combustible."

Label: **Mechanical comparison parallelism / rule of three**. Three consecutive sentences catalogue the results of Keiran's words in a structured list — archer, big man, group tension — each advancing a single variable. The sentences are doing inventory rather than narrating. The third is especially diagnostic: it reaches for atmospheric summarisation after two concrete beats, exactly as AI tends to close a triad.

Suggested rewrite: Collapse the first two into action and let the third absorb the atmosphere: "The arrow came down. The big man moved back. Whatever Keiran had said, the slope stopped feeling like the few seconds before something went wrong."

---

**Hit 2:**
> "He did not know this man. He did not know this land, this group, or what waited below. He had one night's water left, his ribs were sending a throbbing pain with every breath, and the only leverage he had was a dead pig."

Label: **Mechanical comparison parallelism / rule of three (extended)**. The closing paragraph builds a catalogue of three negatives ("did not know... did not know... one night's water... ribs...") in a pattern designed to produce weight through accumulation. The last clause — "the only leverage he had was a dead pig" — functions as the wry thesis payoff, which is structurally identical to how AI assembles a closing beat: list conditions, land the line.

Suggested rewrite: Disrupt the inventory structure. Let one observation lead to another causally rather than additionally: "He had one night's water left, ribs that made breathing an argument, and a dead pig as his only credential in a place where he didn't know the language or the rules."

### Rule of Three

**Hit 1:**
> "It came hard and deep, like something hot had been driven straight through the centre of him and then pulled wide."

Not a list, so not flagged here — but see Narrative Red Flags.

**Hit 2:**
> "caution, suspicion, and the kind of attention that went with someone who needed information more than they needed a fight."

Label: **Rule of three / bullet-point thinking in prose**. Three parallel noun phrases stacked as a single characterisation. "Caution, suspicion, and..." is the standard AI gesture toward completeness — a face-reading that names all three response variables rather than allowing the reader to infer.

Suggested rewrite: Choose the most important one and dramatise it. "She needed something from him — the look made that clear enough — and she was weighing whether to take it."

---

**Hit 3:**
> "You need clean water, time to see to your injuries, and a night that is not spent in open country."

Label: **Rule of three**. Keiran's offer is presented as a three-item list: water, medical, shelter. This is complete and explanatory in a way that feels assembled rather than spoken. Dialogue naturally fragments this.

Suggested rewrite: "You need water. And somewhere to put those ribs." The third item (shelter) is implied by the waystation already mentioned.

---

### Uniform Sentence Length

**Hit 1:**
> "The first figure appeared over the rise at about thirty yards, and the rest followed in that same loose practical spacing he had watched from above. Six. Armed and not casual about it. One had an arrow nocked already. Another had a hand resting on a sword hilt without drawing it, which he decided to read as moderately encouraging."

Label: **Uniform sentence length drift**. The sequence "Six. Armed and not casual about it. One had an arrow nocked already." delivers three near-equal beats in immediate succession. The two-word sentence followed by the six-word sentence followed by the six-word sentence creates a metronomic rhythm that has the cadence of deliberate stylistic control rather than recorded observation.

Suggested rewrite: Break the meter by collapsing two beats. "Six of them, armed and not casual about it. One had an arrow already nocked."

---

### Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis Reasoning

**Hit 1:**
> "It was either a practical arrangement or something Adrian did not yet have the language to assess. Both, possibly."

Label: **Thesis-antithesis-synthesis**. The pattern: state option A, state option B, resolve with "both." This is one of the most reliable AI tells in character interiority — balanced conclusion following a two-sided framing, delivered without friction.

Suggested rewrite: Let the ambiguity stay unresolved. "He didn't have enough ground under him yet to know which it was."

---

**Hit 2:**
> "It was either an honest answer or a very well-shaped deflection. Adrian could not yet tell which."

Label: **Thesis-antithesis-synthesis**. Same structure as above — option A, option B, suspension. The pair appearing twice in the same chapter is a strong pattern marker.

Suggested rewrite: "Adrian filed it. He'd know which one it was when it mattered, or he wouldn't."

## Narrative Red Flags

### "Telling" Emotions with Clinical Precision

**Hit 1:**
> "The adrenaline was there, the panic, the stress, but he also noticed some strange exitement and rush of accomplishment. After all those years of struggling and barely being in control of his body. Those fights actually made him feel alive."

Label: **Clinical emotion labelling / explicit protagonist growth statement**. This is the single most diagnostic passage in the chapter. It breaks from the established free-indirect style to name four discrete emotional states (adrenaline, panic, stress, excitement), then delivers a direct thematic statement about Adrian's relationship to his body and violence. "Those fights actually made him feel alive" is a thesis sentence, not a character moment. The shift in register — from present-tense action to reflective summary — is an AI habit of ensuring emotional content is legible.

Suggested rewrite: Cut the explanatory sentences entirely. If the moment needs to register, let it come through the body: "He was still shaking. He was also, somehow, grinning. He stopped when he noticed."

---

### Every Scene Resolves on a Moral or Emotional Lesson

**Hit 1:**
> "Desperation and good instincts are not so different in practice."

Label: **Scene resolution as lesson**. Keiran's line closes an exchange with a thematic aphorism. The moment functions less as character dialogue and more as an authorial statement about the nature of survival. The line is clean enough to survive, but it is doing the work of a chapter epigraph inside dialogue.

Suggested rewrite: Remove the generalisation. Have Keiran respond only to the specific: "You had your fingers in the wound when I came over the rise. That was not desperation. That was a decision."

---

### Protagonist Growth Signaled by Explicit Statement

**Hit 1:** (same as "telling emotions" above — see that flag)

Additional instance:
> "He adjusted his grip on the spear and kept walking."

Label: Minor — this is close to acceptable, but the final sentence functions as a resolved character beat: Adrian has assessed the situation, decided to trust provisionally, and the action confirms the internal conclusion. It is structurally identical to chapter-close beats generated to signal "protagonist makes a choice and moves forward." The sentence itself is good; its position and function are the flag.

Suggested rewrite: End one beat earlier, or let the last image be environmental rather than action-confirming. "The light had gone gold across the hillside and the mountains were darker than they had been."

## Formatting and Rhythm Tells

### Em-Dash Count

Em-dashes tallied across the chapter: approximately 9.

Instances: "wet and foul enough to make him gag—", "dead mass and failing muscle spread across his chest and legs—", "caution, suspicion, and the kind of attention—", "*Flanking. They are actually flanking me over a dead pig.*" (internal, italics), "which was not aggressive exactly, but was not encouraging either—", "briefly, with genuine interest, and without urgency—", two in Keiran's late dialogue, one in the closing inventory paragraph.

Label: **Em-dash overuse**. Nine is above the six-flag threshold. The density is not severe, but it is consistent with AI prose that reaches for the em-dash to manage rhythm across long descriptive sentences.

Recommended: Review every instance and ask whether a period or restructured sentence would be cleaner. The em-dash in "dead mass and failing muscle spread across his chest and legs, like a collapsed wall of meat and scale" is doing work that a comma pause would handle without the rhythmic interrupt.

---

### Over-Symmetrical Paragraph Endings

**Hit 1:**
> "He adjusted his grip on the spear and kept walking." (final line)

> "Work with that." (internal monologue resolution mid-chapter)

> "Progress." (internal monologue resolution)

Label: **Over-symmetrical paragraph endings**. Three separate internal-monologue beats resolve on a single word or declarative summary. "Progress." / "Work with that." / "All right" / "He adjusted his grip and kept walking." These all function as the same paragraph-closing gesture: assess, conclude briefly, move. Three is above the threshold for consecutive pattern recognition.

Suggested rewrite: Vary the register of at least two. Let one resolution be an unanswered question, or let one be interrupted by sensory detail before the thought resolves.

## Dialogue Tells

### Characters Explaining Things They Already Know

**Hit 1:**
> "They are cautious about you," he said. "You arrived from a dead route wearing salvage that has not been current in a very long time. You carry no local marks. You are also comprehensively covered in blood, which does not help the first impression."

Label: **Explaining for the reader's benefit**. Keiran is telling Adrian things that either Adrian already knows (he is covered in blood) or that Keiran would have no reason to enumerate to Adrian specifically — this information is for the reader. The list of reasons reads as a digest of the situation rather than something a character would actually say.

Suggested rewrite: Compress and redirect toward what Adrian doesn't know. "'They're not sure what to do with you. The blood doesn't help, but that's the smaller problem.' He didn't elaborate on what the larger one was."

---

### Every Character Speaks in Complete Grammatical Sentences

**Hit 1:**

Keiran's entire dialogue runs in complete, grammatically intact sentences with no fragmentation, restart, or interrupt. Sample:

> "The route you came through has been dead for longer than this country has had a name. And you arrived on foot, carrying salvage and a spear, with no escort, no local knowledge, and enough practical sense to assess a group of armed strangers before walking into range."

> "They are continuing toward the waystation below the ridge. Approximately two hours. Defensible. Supplied."

The second example breaks into fragments, but they are controlled, deliberate fragments — all parallel nouns, all completing the same sentence. It is list-fragmentation rather than natural speech rhythm.

Label: **Complete grammatical sentences / no natural fragmentation**. Keiran never trails off, revises mid-thought, or produces an incomplete sentence that the context doesn't immediately resolve. His speech is clean in a way that works for the character archetype but should be stress-tested: would even a composed, experienced person provide this level of tidy syntax in informal conversation?

Suggested rewrite: Give him one false start or one redirect. "'You need water, time for—' He glanced at Adrian's coat. 'And a night that's not out here.'"
