**Verdict**

The chapter's story logic holds in its broad architecture: a man waking into an incomprehensible world observes carefully, behaves consistently with his established psychology, and accumulates information while managing social position under uncertainty. The scene-to-scene causation is largely sound. However, two structural problems erode the chapter's credibility as a real sequence of events. First, the Empty Trait revelation lands with theatrical precision — timed, staged, and socially complete — in a way that outstrips what the scene has actually prepared. Second, the Hunt event arrives as a pure system announcement with no situational grounding, and the group's response to it is emotionally coherent but informationally hollow, leaving Adrian (and the reader) floating in abstraction at the chapter's most critical beat. Neither problem is fatal, but both ask the reader to accept convenient dramatic machinery in place of earned consequence.

**Beat-by-Beat Analysis**

---

**"He went through the door last, waited until the heavy slab of it had thudded shut behind him, and put his back to the nearest section of wall where he could see the hearth, the entrance, and most of the room without needing to move much."**

*Logic: sound.* This is entirely consistent with Adrian's established behaviour across prior chapters — the automatic tactical positioning, the reading of exits and angles, the conservation of movement. No flag.

---

**"They were young. Not in the vague sense where anyone under forty looked young when your day had involved cryostasis, teleportation, and a mutant boar. Actually young."**

*Logic: sound.* Adrian's observation that this group is inexperienced, and his subsequent reading of the micro-corrections in their behaviour, is credible given his established pattern of reading people through physical tells and situational evidence. The inference about first ranging comes later and is properly earned by this groundwork. No flag.

---

**"The big man straightened, rolled one shoulder as if easing old strain out of it, and looked round the room before his eyes landed on Adrian... He nudged the bundle across the stone with two fingers."**

*Logic: sound.* The ambiguity of the gesture — hospitality, test, or frontier utility — is handled well. Adrian's decision to behave as if all three are simultaneously true is consistent with his established pragmatism under uncertainty. No flag.

---

**"Then, annoyingly, his attention caught on the details properly... The certainty was thin, provisional, barely worthy of the word instinct, but it had not belonged to him thirty seconds earlier."**

*Logic: minor flag.* The description of the skill acquisition is plausible in isolation, but the chapter has Adrian cooking for the first time on an unfamiliar stone with unfamiliar meat over a fire he has never used. The system registers this as a skill event triggered by a single, failed attempt. What the chapter has not established is why *this* particular action crosses the system's recognition threshold when earlier actions — fighting, physical labour, tactical navigation — apparently did not produce comparable notifications. The chapter's own explanation of skill recognition (repeated action, honest engagement, enough substance) slightly undercuts the speed of this acquisition. The skill itself is Inferior and the chapter acknowledges this, which softens the problem, but the mechanism asks more from the reader than it has prepared.

*What it assumes:* That a single cooking attempt, however attentive, is enough for system recognition.
*What it has established:* That skill recognition requires pattern and repetition.

*Suggestion:* Either have the system's explanation come before the event (so the rule is in place before it fires) or have a brief beat where Adrian recognises he has been doing something analogous unconsciously — sharpening the knife, managing the fire, arranging the cuts — long enough to constitute the pattern the system needs. Even a sentence of this would close the gap.

---

**"He asks if the system marked it... He wants the grade... Inferior... which feels fair."**

*Logic: sound.* The exchange is credible. The young man's enthusiasm about system recognition is consistent with someone newly awakened and still engaged with its mechanics. The warmth this creates in the room follows plausibly from the established dynamic of young people who share a framework Adrian is visibly trying to learn. No flag.

---

**"She says your Trait registration is empty. That sentence altered the room at once."**

*Logic: flag — this is the chapter's most significant story-logic problem.*

The revelation is staged with theatrical precision: the healer completes the healing, lingers, registers something wrong, reports it, and the room responds with a coordinated silent shift that involves every named character in sequence. The problem is not that the reaction is disproportionate — the chapter later establishes that an empty trait is genuinely alarming in this world, which is sound. The problem is the mechanism of discovery.

*What it assumes:* That a healer's diagnostic touch routinely reads class and trait registration, and that this information would be legible to her as part of standard wound assessment.

*What it has established:* Nothing. The healer has been introduced as someone who treats physical damage. There has been no prior indication that healing in this world involves or requires reading system registration, or that a practitioner's trait is visible to another practitioner through touch. The healer does not react to his Fighter class — only to the empty Trait. If she can read registration through contact, why is this the first time this is relevant? If reading registration is standard practice among healers, why has no one in the prior chapters signalled this as a capability?

The chapter also times this revelation to arrive immediately after the room has warmed to Adrian (the cooking exchange, the laugh, the loosened shoulders). The before-and-after is structurally clean to the point of feeling arranged. Story logic does not prohibit dramatically clean reversals, but this one rests entirely on a capability that has been invented for the purpose of the scene.

*Specific suggestion:* Either establish earlier — on the slope, or at the waystation door — that healers in this world conduct a basic registration scan as part of triage (a brief line of Keiran explaining this, or a moment where the healer pauses during initial wound assessment and Adrian wonders what she's reading), or have the revelation arrive through a different mechanism that has been seeded (the system itself surfacing the information in response to a new social context, or another character asking Adrian directly about his trait registration as part of the group's assessment of him). The revelation itself is the right dramatic beat. The delivery mechanism needs to be earned.

---

**"He had known the word Empty since the system first showed it to him four days ago."**

*Logic: flag — continuity against prior chapters.* This line states that Adrian has known about the Empty Trait for four days. The prior chapter context indicates his awakening was recent and the system interface has been a recurring element. If this information has been present since near the beginning and Adrian has been treating it as "a technical failure, an ugly blank," the chapter needs to account for why he has not previously sought to understand or address it. The chapter does address this partially — "he had treated it like a technical failure" — but it does not explain why, given his established intelligence and situational awareness, he never asked Keiran about it before now. Keiran has been available. Adrian knows Keiran has knowledge. The Empty Trait is a significant system anomaly. The chapter asks us to accept that a careful, observant man ignored a conspicuous problem in his interface for four days until a healer forced it into public view.

*What it assumes:* That Adrian's incuriosity about the Empty Trait was sustained long enough to make this a fresh revelation to him.

*What it has established:* A man who reads situations carefully, asks questions when he has the opportunity, and identifies what information he needs.

*Suggestion:* Either have Adrian acknowledge that he has been deliberately avoiding the question (establish why — denial, more pressing survival priorities, reluctance to draw attention to something that might mark him as anomalous), or have him already know it is a social problem and have been actively managing that knowledge before the healer exposes it. Either version is psychologically plausible. The current version leaves a gap in his information-management behaviour.

---

**"The big man appeared in the low doorway at the back as if he had heard the tone more than the words."**

*Logic: sound.* The big man's response — appearing, doing quick arithmetic, withdrawing without drama — is the most credible reaction in the sequence. His frontier pragmatism is consistent with everything established about him. No flag.

---

**"He tried to map the logic of it from what he had already learned. Class gave the broad shape. Trait was the seeded second layer..."**

*Logic: sound.* Adrian's reasoning about the structural implications of an empty trait is consistent with his established pattern of working problems analytically when he lacks direct information. No flag.

---

**"[System Event Pending] [The Hunt]"**

*Logic: flag — the chapter's second critical problem, addressed below.*

The Hunt notification arrives without any situational grounding. There is no prior indication that sealed-boundary events exist as a system mechanic, no hint that the area they have entered carries elevated risk of such events, and no suggestion from Keiran or anyone else that "first ranging" routes sometimes coincide with predator events. The notification lands as a pure system announcement — arbitrary from inside the story's world — and the chapter has nothing to set against it except the group's fear.

*What it assumes:* That system-initiated sealed events are a known mechanic of this world that does not require any prior setup.

*What it has established:* That the system announces itself through prompts, that first ranging is a monitored frontier exercise, and that Keiran has some knowledge of how this world works. Nothing about sealed-boundary predator events, their frequency, or their triggers.

The problem is compounded by Adrian's response. He reads the notification, registers its terms, and proceeds to analyse it. But he has no framework for evaluating "sealed boundary," "active participants," "predator event," or "event duration: 30 days" against anything real in this world. His analysis is therefore floating — he identifies the clauses as bad without being able to assess what any of them mean in practice. This is honest writing, but it leaves the chapter's closing section resting on atmosphere (the group's fear, Keiran's barely-suppressed alarm) rather than any concrete understanding of stakes.

*Specific suggestion:* Seed the possibility of events before the notification fires. This does not require a heavy hand — a single line from Keiran earlier in the waystation scene about why waystation routes are monitored, or a moment where the big man checks something about their location that a reader can retroactively understand as event-boundary awareness, would be sufficient. If the reader can look back when the notification arrives and recognise that the setup was there, the event stops being an arbitrary system announcement and becomes a consequence of where these people chose to be.

**"Keiran had not moved much, which was exactly why Adrian watched him harder... That mattered more than the group's fear did."**

*Logic: sound.* Adrian's calibration of Keiran's reaction as more meaningful than the group's is consistent with his established pattern of reading experienced operators differently from novices. His reasoning — that fear from inexperienced people is noise but a controlled reaction from a knowledgeable person is signal — is psychologically and tactically credible. No flag.

---

**"Thirty days. Twelve hours before it began. Less than half a day to learn the rules of a trap he had not chosen..."**

*Logic: sound in spirit, incomplete in execution.* Adrian's closing analysis is consistent with his character. The problem is that it is entirely abstract. He identifies five variables (boundary size, predator, rules of engagement, secondary objectives, group reliability) but has no concrete information about any of them, and the chapter ends without him taking any action to reduce that ignorance. Given that he has twelve hours and is in a room with people who understand the system better than he does, and given that Keiran has just told him "the next twelve hours matter," Adrian's inaction — sitting by the fire, turning things over — reads as slightly under-calibrated to the urgency the chapter has established.

*What it assumes:* That "twelve hours matter" is sufficient to end the chapter without Adrian beginning to act on that information.

*What it has established:* A man who, under pressure, moves toward information rather than away from it.

*Suggestion:* Either have the chapter end slightly earlier, before Keiran's "the next twelve hours matter" line establishes urgency, or have Adrian begin the process of acting on it — a question to Keiran, a request for information, an attempt to open negotiation with the big man about what the event requires. Even a failed attempt would be consistent with his character. The chapter does not need to resolve his plan; it needs him to start forming one.

---

**Two Most Critical Story-Logic Problems**

**1. The healer's diagnostic revelation of the Empty Trait.**

This is the chapter's structural pivot — everything before it is establishment, everything after it is consequence — and it rests on a capability (a healer reading system registration through touch) that has been introduced purely to serve this scene. The dramatic effect is correct. The mechanism is invented. If the healer's ability to read registration is a standard feature of healing in this world, it requires prior grounding — even a sentence. If it is not standard but something she has noticed anomalously, that needs to be the chapter's framing, not a flat report. As currently constructed, the most important revelation in the chapter arrives through a door that was not there before the chapter needed it.

**2. The Hunt event arrives without situational preparation.**

A thirty-day sealed predator event that overrides a monitored first-ranging route is the chapter's climactic turn. It is presented as a system announcement with no prior seeding of the conditions that make it possible or likely. The group's fear is credible. Keiran's reaction is credible. But neither substitutes for the reader having any sense of why this happened here, now, to these people. "The system did it" is not a story-logic explanation; it is the absence of one. The event needs at least one prior detail — geographic, mechanical, or situational — that makes its arrival feel like consequence rather than authorial intervention. Without it, the chapter closes on a crisis that has been delivered rather than earned.
