**Verdict**

This chapter is substantially better than it has any right to be this far into a first draft. The waystation atmosphere is earned, the social dynamics of the group are handled with intelligence, and the Empty reveal lands with real structural force. The problems are real but they are local: hollow action lines cluster in three or four passages and undermine scenes that are otherwise working; there are two or three simile-by-assembly moments that break the POV voice; the Keiran exchange runs slightly long on restraint and slightly short on revelation; and the system prompt at the end arrives before the room's internal tension has been allowed to breathe. None of these are structural collapses. They are prose failures that a line pass can fix — provided the pass is honest.

---

**SECTION 1: The opening paragraph**

> "The walls were thick enough to keep weather and arrows away. The windows were little more than slits. The roof sagged in no visible place and still carried the dull weight of old repairs."

Three short parallel sentences used for symmetry, not necessity. Each one delivers one feature in identical grammatical packaging. This is assembly, not observation. Adrian does not perceive architecture in a list. The compression that follows — *nobody had built it to be welcoming* — is the actual sentence. Everything before it is throat-clearing.

Rewrite:
*"The walls were thick enough to stop arrows, the windows barely wide enough to look through, and the roof had that particular settled permanence of something that had been repaired often enough to stop failing."*

Or, simpler: cut to what works.

*"The waystation sat low against the hillside, squat dark stone half-swallowed by the slope — built to hold heat, deny sight-lines, and give travellers one hard shell between themselves and whatever ranged the March after dark."*

The three-feature list structure appears again later in the chapter. It is a tic. Flag it now and comb for it everywhere.

---

**SECTION 2: Adrian entering the room**

> "He went through the door last, waited until the heavy slab of it had thudded shut behind him, and put his back to the nearest section of wall where he could see the hearth, the entrance, and most of the room without needing to move much."

"nearest section of wall" is padding. Nobody puts their back to a section of a wall. They put their back to the wall.

Minor. Fix on pass.

---

**SECTION 3: Body inventory paragraph**

> "His body had been asking him to stop for the better part of the past hour. The pull in his ribs had settled into a steady warning that sharpened every time he forgot and breathed too deeply. His knee felt hot and uncertain under his weight, as if one bad twist might make an argument of it."

This works. "Make an argument of it" is a good verb construction — the knee as a thing with grievances. This is the right register for Adrian's voice. Leave it.

---

**SECTION 4: The hallucination paragraph**

> "Smoke, damp stone, old grease, waxed cloth, mud drying on boots by the door. It was all too specific. The details kept lining up against the easier answers. If this was a hallucination, it had an exhausting commitment to texture. If it was real, then he was alive inside it whether he understood any of it or not."

This is good. The parallelism here is earned because the two "if" clauses are doing different work. Do not touch it.

---

**SECTION 5: Watching the group settle — hollow action cluster**

> "One boy took the door and checked the latch before setting himself beside it. Two more knelt by the hearth, and Adrian saw one glance at the other before touching flame to kindling, as though silently confirming the order of things."

"Adrian saw" is a hollow frame that tells us he is watching without putting him in the act of watching. It creates distance between the POV and the observation at the exact moment close observation is the point.

Rewrite:
*"Two more knelt by the hearth — one glanced at the other before touching flame to kindling, a small check, the kind people made when the order of things hadn't been spoken aloud yet but everyone expected it to exist."*

> "The big man disappeared through a low back door without asking anyone whether he should."

This is efficient. Keep it.

> "Nobody ignored Adrian by accident. Every time somebody crossed the room, their eyes clipped over him for a beat."

"clipped over him" is good. This is the right choice.

---

**SECTION 6: "He stayed where he was and watched while his breathing eased back under control."**

Hollow action line. "Watched" without object, content, or texture. The watching is the point of the scene, so this verb needs to carry weight.

Rewrite:
*"He stayed where he was and let his breathing settle, counting faces while it did."*

---

**SECTION 7: The big man and the meat — hollow action cluster**

> "The big man returned carrying a cloth-wrapped bundle tucked under one arm. He set it on the stone by the hearth, unrolled the cloth, and exposed a slab of dark meat with the efficient indifference of someone more interested in utility than ceremony."

"Exposed" is the wrong verb here — it implies concealment was the prior state. "Laid out" or simply "put" the meat on the stone. The phrase "efficient indifference of someone more interested in utility than ceremony" is accurate but over-explained. The reader sees this in "He said nothing, offered no smile" two sentences later. You are saying the same thing twice.

Cut the characterisation from the action line and let the next paragraph carry it:

*"The big man returned, set a cloth-wrapped bundle on the stone by the hearth, and unrolled it. Dark meat, leaner than pork, faint bluish tinge at one cut edge."*

> "Then he nudged the bundle across the stone with two fingers."

Hollow action line immediately following another action line. Two consecutive short sentences describing physical placement of an object. Neither carries Adrian's perception of what is happening.

Rewrite:
*"Then he nudged the bundle across the stone with two fingers — not offering it, exactly. More like placing it in front of someone and waiting to see what they'd do with it."*

---

**SECTION 8: "It could have been hospitality. It could have been a test. It could have been the simple frontier logic..."**

Three parallel constructions again. This one is justified — it is internal reasoning, Adrian working through possibilities, and the rhythm of the triple does actual work here. However, the following sentence:

> "Adrian had no way to choose between them, which meant it made sense to behave as if all three were true."

This makes the triple redundant in retrospect. The conclusion explains the reasoning, which means the reader sees all three options and the resolution. That is correct structure. Keep it. But consider whether the third option — "simple frontier logic that an extra pair of hands ought to earn its heat" — needs the phrase "the simple frontier logic." It signals the thought is about to be interesting before it actually is.

Minor.

---

**SECTION 9: The cooking skill acquisition**

This sequence works well. The internal logic of the skill appearing mid-task is convincing, the voice is right, and:

> *Right. I burn it once, I learn it once. Wonderful.*

This is good italicised interiority. The rhythm lands. Leave it.

The moment of cognitive shift before the prompt is also handled correctly:

> "It was not knowledge he had earned enough to trust, only a thin extra layer of certainty dropped onto the work in front of him."

This is the best sentence in the chapter. It characterises the system's behaviour better than any explicit explanation could.

---

**SECTION 10: Passing the food round — hollow action cluster**

> "He used the knife tip to shift the least charred pieces onto scraps of cloth and passed them round one by one, watching each face with more attention than the situation strictly deserved."

This is fine. The qualification — "more attention than the situation strictly deserved" — keeps Adrian present as a perceiver.

> "Nobody sniffed suspiciously. Nobody handed theirs back."

Good deadpan. Keep it.

---

**SECTION 11: The young man's reaction to the skill grade**

> "Whatever Keiran translated made the young man's expression do something complicated and unexpectedly kind."

"Do something complicated" is placeholder prose. It tells the reader a thing happened to the expression without describing the thing. If you do not have the specific detail, the sentence must be cut or restructured so it performs a different function.

Rewrite:
*"Something in Keiran's translation reached the young man and sat there for a moment — not pity, but something adjacent to recognition."*

Or cut it entirely and let the speech that follows ("everyone starts somewhere") carry the tone without preamble.

---

**SECTION 12: Worldbuilding explanation of the passive skill**

The explanation of how passives work — *a recognised passive was not just being vaguely good at something* — is handled through indirect dialogue, which is the right call. The information is necessary and the staging earns it. However, the explanation paragraph is slightly front-loaded with abstraction before it gets concrete:

> "A recognised passive was not just being vaguely good at something. It meant the system had accepted a pattern as real: repeated action, honest engagement, enough substance that it could begin fixing the behaviour into something stable."

"Honest engagement" is vague. What does the system know about honesty? This should be Adrian's framing of what he has just been told. Adjust to reflect his voice:

*"A recognised passive was not just being vaguely good at something. The system had to see a pattern: enough repeated action under real conditions, enough adjustment, before it would start treating the behaviour as fixed."*

---

**SECTION 13: First ranging explanation**

> "The closest Common equivalent to the name was first ranging."

"Common" — is this the name for the shared language? If so, this is the first time it has been named and it arrives without any frame. The reader has to infer it from context. That inference is possible but slightly bumpy. Either name it earlier in the chapter in a natural context, or replace "Common" here with a construction that does not rely on prior knowledge:

*"The closest equivalent in his language was first ranging."*

---

**SECTION 14: The Empty reveal — room reaction**

This is the chapter's best scene and it is structurally correct. The group reaction is observed from the outside, which is the right choice — Adrian does not have interiority to offer here because he already knows about Empty. His job is to read the room.

> "The young man's shoulders eased back by a fraction, as though instinct had told him to create space before his mind caught up."

Good.

> "The woman in the mended coat stopped with one buckle half-threaded through a strap."

Good. This is exactly the right level of specificity.

> "The big man appeared in the low doorway at the back as if he had heard the tone more than the words."

Good. "The tone more than the words" earns its place.

> "His expression did not sharpen because it did not need to. It had already settled into a kind of frontier practicality Adrian was beginning to recognise: not cruelty, not fear exactly, but the fast internal arithmetic of risk, cost, and whether a problem was likely to become expensive."

"Fast internal arithmetic" is a good construction. The paragraph as a whole delivers the characterisation, which is why the following line is slightly redundant:

> "He said something flat. Keiran answered in much the same register. Adrian did not need the language to know what kind of exchange it was. This was not curiosity. This was classification."

"This was not curiosity. This was classification." — the two-sentence beat is meant to land hard, and it does, but "Adrian did not need the language to know what kind of exchange it was" is doing the same work. Cut the setup sentence:

*"He said something flat. Keiran answered in much the same register. This was not curiosity. This was classification."*

**SECTION 15: The conversation with Keiran — pacing**

> "Is any of this real, and am I actually alive?"

This is the right question to ask, and placing it here — after the Empty reveal, after the room has changed — gives it weight. The problem is that Keiran's responses have been accumulating the same register since he first appeared. Every exchange is:
- Adrian presses
- Keiran gives partial answer in clipped phrase
- Adrian recognises the answer is incomplete
- Keiran confirms with another partial answer

This rhythm is used six times in this conversation alone. The register is correct for Keiran, but the repetition dulls it. At least one exchange needs to break the pattern — either Keiran volunteers something unprompted, or Adrian refuses to accept the partial answer and Keiran gives slightly more than usual. As written, the conversation accumulates information without advancing the dynamic between them, which means it is doing worldbuilding work that could have been done in half the space.

The one exception:

> "You are standing in a place that inherited it."

This is the best line Keiran has. It is stranger than the others, less resolute, and it opens more than it closes. More of this.

---

**SECTION 16: The healer scene before the reveal**

> "Adrian peeled the coat off with less grace than he would have preferred."

Good. Specific, physically grounded, tells us something about Adrian.

> "It did not feel like ordinary heat. It was more exact than that. Something moved inward along the damage and pressed into it with quiet, deliberate purpose, finding the places that had been catching every time he breathed too deeply and smoothing the sharpest edges away."

This is good physical description of a system ability. The sentence "finding the places that had been catching every time he breathed too deeply" is doing double duty — recalling established pain and describing healing simultaneously.

> "Whatever else this world turned out to be, his ribs had an opinion on whether he was alive in it."

This is good.

> "Being fussed over would have been harder."

Good. Character-consistent and efficiently placed.

---

**SECTION 17: The Empty interiority**

> "He had known the word Empty since the system first showed it to him four days ago."

Wait — four days ago? The chapter context says Adrian has just arrived; previous chapters establish he went through the arch recently. If "four days ago" is accurate within the story's timeline, that needs clarification, because the reader may be counting. If it is an error, correct it.

> "In this room it stopped being technical. It was the thing that made a laugh vanish, made the big man do quick sums in the doorway, and taught everyone else's eyes to linger on him a second longer than comfort allowed."

Good. The three clauses here earn their parallel structure because each one refers to a specific thing the reader just witnessed.

> "No inherited bias. No obvious line of development. Maybe no trustworthy prediction about where anything would go."

Three short parallel sentences again, this time as fragments. This one is borderline. The fragment structure suggests clipped thinking under pressure, which is appropriate here. It works if you accept that Adrian thinks in short bursts when examining a problem. Borderline justifiable. Leave it but note it as a tic.

---

**SECTION 18: The Hunt prompt and closing**

> "Adrian read the title once and muttered, 'That's never encouraging.'"

"That's never encouraging" is generic. This is a title. Adrian knows nothing about what *The Hunt* means. A man who has spent the chapter reasoning carefully from detail would not land on a generic aphorism. He would react more specifically to the shape of the information.

Rewrite:
*"Adrian read the title once. Titles were either literal or worse than literal, and he had no good reason to assume this one was going to surprise him."*

Or cut the muttered comment entirely and let him open it.

> "Sealed boundary and departure restricted were bad enough, active participants made it worse, and thirty days made it absurd."

Good. Adrian's voice is right here.

> "That sounded less like apprenticeship and more like being informed by bureaucracy that a disaster had already been filed."

"Being informed by bureaucracy that a disaster had already been filed" is the second-best sentence in the chapter. Keep it.

The closing sequence of the chapter — Adrian watching the group react, watching Keiran, arriving at his analysis — is structurally correct. The chapter ends with him staring at the fire inside a sealed event, underpowered and undersocialised, having just watched the room change against him. That is the right landing for this chapter's arc. The problem is the final paragraph:

> "The fire cracked softly in the middle of the silence, absurdly normal, and Adrian found himself staring at it while the numbers settled into place."

"Found himself staring" is hollow. He did not find himself doing anything — he is the POV. He is staring or he is not. The phrase "found himself" removes him from his own action.

> "Thirty days. Twelve hours before it began. Less than half a day to learn the rules of a trap he had not chosen, work out why it had closed on this route, and decide whether these people were going to be his best chance of surviving it or one more problem inside the boundary."

This closing paragraph is functional but it over-explains. The chapter's final work is being done in summary: Adrian lists what he knows and does not know. The reader already knows all of this. A closing beat that does not restate the problem but instead drops him into one specific sensation or image would land harder.

Consider:
*"The fire cracked softly. Thirty days. He looked at the young man across the hearth, who was looking at his own hands, and did not think about what that meant yet."*

---

**THREE BIGGEST STRUCTURAL PROBLEMS, RANKED**

**1. Hollow action lines — clusters in the early and middle sections.**
They appear most densely in the first half of the chapter, before the cooking sequence restores physical presence to the prose. The sections where Adrian is observing the group settle into the waystation are the worst offenders. This is not a single fix — it requires a full pass with the hollow-line criterion applied sentence by sentence. Wherever Adrian watches, sees, stays, or takes something without a sensory or cognitive frame, the sentence either needs a detail or needs to be cut and the next beat allowed to carry the weight.

**2. The Keiran dialogue runs on a single rhythm for too long.**
Every exchange follows the same partial-answer pattern. The accumulation of information is correct but the dynamic between the two characters does not develop across the conversation. Keiran should be slightly more exposed at least once — not more revelatory, but more specifically evasive, in a way that tells Adrian (and the reader) something different about the shape of what he is hiding. "You are standing in a place that inherited it" already does this; the surrounding lines do not. Cut or compress the exchanges that simply confirm evasiveness and let the one or two lines where something genuinely strange surfaces do more work.

**3. The closing paragraph fails to land the chapter.**
The final image returns Adrian to passive summary exactly when the narrative has built enough pressure to earn a specific, grounded, forward-facing beat. "The numbers settled into place" is the weakest possible ending for a chapter that has been, at its best, precise about sensation and inference. The last sentence needs to carry physical presence, not arithmetic. Replace the summary with a single specific detail — a sound, a gesture from across the room, a thing Adrian notices and does not yet understand — and let the chapter end on that. The reader will carry the numbers themselves.
