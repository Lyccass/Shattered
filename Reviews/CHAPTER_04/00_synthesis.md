## HARD BANS

### H1 — Exposition precedes its trigger
**Source:** Style Auditor (Flag 4.1)
**Quote:** `"People made caches when they had time, fear, access to tools, and very little interest in dying empty-handed."`
**Issue:** Generalised principle arrives in anticipation of the panel opening, not as a response to what Adrian sees inside it. Exposition must be triggered by the immediate scene.
**Fix:** Move this sentence to after the panel opens and Adrian sees the organised compartment. The visible structure is the trigger; the principle should follow it.
- [x] Applied

## STORY LOGIC

### S1 — The possible voice is processed as ambient threat (CRITICAL)
**Source:** Story Analyst (Beat 22), Writing Professional (Series Viability), AI Detector (Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis Hit 1)
**Quote:** `"It could have been survivors, or something with hands, or a door somewhere finally deciding to act like a door again, and none of those possibilities made the room around him feel any safer."`
**Issue:** The possibility of another living person is assigned equal weight to a malfunctioning door. Adrian has had no human contact since waking. His mother is gone. He said "sorry" to a dead worker. The specific possibility of another person should produce one beat of differentiated attention before he returns to the threat framework — not drama, just the correct interior registration.
**Fix:** One sentence holding the distinction between machinery noise and intentional movement — something made by something that decided to make it — before collapsing back to the threat assessment. The decision to move toward it can remain exactly as is.
- [x] Applied

### S2 — Empty Trait attention disproportionate to physical urgency (CRITICAL)
**Source:** Story Analyst (Beat 14), Writing Professional (Commercial Readability), Royal Road Reader (Engagement)
**Quote:** `"The word Empty sat there like a bad joke, and he focused on it again, partly because he was stubborn and partly because annoyance was doing useful work keeping worse emotions out."`
**Issue:** Adrian is dehydrated, bleeding, and holding a free stat point when he gives the Empty Trait a second extended beat. His established cognitive hierarchy consistently places material survival before abstract existential problems. The return is psychologically sound — it bothers him, he is stubborn — but the framing makes it read as a considered re-examination. It should read as a compulsive return he partly resents making; something that gets in despite his better judgment.
**Fix:** Keep the beat, shrink the framing, and make the compulsive quality explicit. He cannot stop himself from looking at it even though he knows he should be moving. The acknowledgement that he is "stubborn" is a step toward this but reads as authorial note rather than felt recognition.
- [x] Applied

### S3 — Hatchet rejection over-deliberated
**Source:** Story Analyst (Beat 17)
**Quote:** `"He hefted it once, liking the straightforward weight of it, then imagined one of the wall-things slipping past the first swing and arriving inside that short arc with teeth and claws and momentum."`
**Issue:** He has just lived a fight where he repeatedly lost the close-range exchange. The hatchet's failure scenario should be immediate recognition, not simulation. He lived the failure with the maintenance pipe.
**Fix:** The hatchet rejection should be faster — one or two sentences of recognition rather than an imagined scenario. The chapter can still explain the logic, but the felt speed of the dismissal should reflect his visceral education.
- [x] Applied

### S4 — Stat allocation continuity error (STRUCTURAL)
**Source:** Lector (Section 16)
**Quote:** `"He opened the status again before his mind found something less useful to obsess over, and the free point still waited there with calm administrative patience."`
**Issue:** Adrian spent the free point on Agility in the preceding section, and the chapter confirmed the physical effect. "Still waited" implies the point has not been spent, contradicting what just happened. This reads as the same point being spent twice.
**Fix:** The analysis, decision, and physical confirmation of the Agility allocation must form one linear sequence. If wound treatment is meant to separate decision from allocation, the "still waited" language must be revised to reflect a decision already made — or the second status check must be repositioned to before the wound treatment.
- [x] Applied

### S5 — Will's anomaly is raised and immediately abandoned
**Source:** Lector (Section 16, Structural Problem #3)
**Quote:** `"Will remained oddly high and completely uninterested in explaining itself."`
**Issue:** Will is Adrian's joint-highest base stat, did not increase, and is flagged as anomalous. Adrian is an analytical character who draws conclusions from anomalies. He identifies this one and immediately shelves it with no theory, no physical manifestation, no inference. As written it is a placeholder, not a beat.
**Fix:** Either give Adrian a theory — even a wrong one — about why Will sits where it does, or introduce a physical or behavioural manifestation earlier in the chapter that the observation can retroactively explain. Raising an anomaly and choosing not to engage with it at all is the pattern that has followed the Empty Trait across chapters.
- [ ] Not applied — planned for Chapter 5

## STRUCTURE

### ST1 — Panic spiral covers the same ground three times (CRITICAL)
**Source:** Lector (Structural Problem #1)
**Sections affected:** Shaking hands / aftermath spiral → Breathing exercise → "Useful problems" reframe → Thirst passage
**Issue:** Sections 3 through 7 all deliver the same beat: Adrian is destabilised, he resists collapse, he returns to methodical thinking. The illness-relief moment — the chapter's most important emotional beat — is currently buried under two re-statements of the same reframe. The first pass (shaking hands + illness relief) needs room; the repetition takes it away.
**Fix:** Cut or compress the "Nope. Absolutely not." reasoning chain and the "Useful problems" restatement in the thirst paragraph. The logic chain in the spiral section does this work; restating it via "That did not solve anything. It did not get him water..." is redundant. The physical details of thirst and hunger are good — they ground the scene — but the drawn conclusion has already been made twice.
- [x] Applied

### ST2 — Weapon selection repeats identical structural shape four times
**Source:** Style Auditor (Flag 1.4), AI Detector (Repeated Structural Mirroring), Lector (Section 12)
**Issue:** Sword, hatchet, hammer, and shield each receive the same paragraph pattern: Adrian picks it up / feels something promising / tests it / rejects it / gives the reason. Four consecutive paragraphs in exact structural sequence. By the shield, the reader has predicted the conclusion before reading the sentence.
**Fix:** Compress the sword rejection to two sentences maximum (it is the archetype of "wrong tool, wrong person" and does not need the full treatment). Let the shield paragraph carry the fuller analysis — the shield is the more interesting failure because Adrian *wants* it to work. The desire earns the longer treatment. Alternatively, break the shield paragraph's structural form entirely: arrive at rejection through a different angle so the fourth item does not echo the first three.
- [x] Applied

### ST3 — "Useful problems" stated three times
**Source:** Lector (Section 7)
**Quote:** `"Fine. Those were useful problems: water, food, a better weapon, something for the wounds. If he kept going one thing at a time, he might stop his brain from trying to sprint in six directions at once."`
**Issue:** This is the third passage to deliver the same conclusion (after the spiral's logic chain and the "What mattered right now" passage). The physical details are good; the drawn conclusion is redundant.
**Fix:** Keep the physical sensory content (dry mouth, hunger, knee complaint). Cut the restatement of the conclusion. End the beat at the sensory content: "Good. Those he could do something about."
- [x] Applied

### L2 — Aftermath passage over-explained (seven sentences → compress)
**Source:** Lector (Section 3)
**Quote:** `"Not the killing, exactly, or even the part where one of the things had nearly gotten into his ribs. It was the delayed, stupidly human aftermath, the point where his body apparently decided the emergency notice could finally go out..."`  through `"He had not been slow because his own body had betrayed him."`
**Issue:** Seven sentences all confirming the illness is gone and that matters. The illness-relief beat is buried in its own explanation. The final two sentences ("He had been slow because he was scared... He had not been slow because his own body had betrayed him.") use parallel structure to weaken both rather than land either.
**Fix (Lector's version):**
> Not the killing, exactly, or even the part where one of the things had nearly gotten into his ribs. It was the delayed, stupidly human aftermath — his body finally sending the emergency notice now that the immediate danger had stopped moving. He looked at his hands, then his left leg, then his hands again. All still working. His knee hurt, his side burned, his pulse had apparently relocated to his throat. But when he had needed to move, he had moved. Not slow. Not betrayed.

## THE WRITING PROFESSIONAL'S QUESTION

*Raise with the author. Do not implement. The author must answer it before the next draft.*

> At the end of Chapter 4, Adrian steps out of a room in which he managed an adrenaline crash, a panic spiral, and a near-breakdown entirely alone, using the tools his particular psychology provides, without needing anyone else and without the absence of anyone else appearing to cost him anything he cannot cover himself.
>
> **Is Adrian Keller's self-sufficiency a character trait the series is building toward testing, or is it the author's own preference for a protagonist who does not require other people — and if the latter, does the author understand what that costs at scale?**
