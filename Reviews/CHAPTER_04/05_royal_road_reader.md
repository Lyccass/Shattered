# Role 2 — The Royal Road Reader

## First Impression

I almost stalled in the first paragraph. "Heat pushed out from the Runeheart beneath Adrian's sternum, not with the crushing violence of the class selection but with a slow, insistent force that spread through shoulders, forearms, spine, hips, and thighs and made every muscle lock for half a heartbeat before releasing." That is one sentence doing a lot of commuting before it arrives. I stayed with it — the image of heat moving through a body in inventory order is specific enough to earn the length — but it asked me to hold a lot of structure in my head before delivering the payoff. The Royal Road first scroll is punishing. I would give that sentence about half a second before deciding whether it was worth the work.

What hooked me was paragraph three. The moment he jams the pipe into the twitching body one last time — "He kept the pipe there for another second anyway, leaning on it harder than he needed to, because he did not yet trust stillness in this place" — that is the exact paranoid competence I like about Adrian. He is not brave. He is just not stupid. I was in by that line.

---

## Character Likability

He is still my favourite thing about this story and this chapter is where I feel it most. The panic spiral in the first third of the chapter — the breathing exercise, the counting, the frank acknowledgement that his lungs had formed their own opinion — is the best version of Adrian yet. Because he does not perform competence through it. He is genuinely a mess. He just refuses to stay one.

The line that got me: "Breathe in, breathe out, repeat until adulthood resumed." That is funny in the way things are funny when they are also true. He is not being witty for an audience. He is being witty at himself to keep from falling apart, and the chapter makes that distinction feel real.

The bit I love most in the whole chapter is the relief about his illness being gone. "He had not been slow because his own body had betrayed him. The relief came off that realisation so hard it nearly folded him." That is the chapter's best moment and it does not even announce itself. It lands and keeps going. After four chapters of only vague backstory about what he had, this is the first time I understood what that illness *meant* to him, and it hits hard because it comes in sideways. I did not expect to feel that here. I wanted more of it.

The weapon selection is also very him. He puts the sword down because it "assumed a man who knew what he was doing." He resents the shield for making too many demands. He laughs when the system descriptor for the spear says "ugly work." That is a character choosing a weapon the way a character would actually choose a weapon rather than the way a plot decides for them. I believed every word.

## Language Accessibility

The chapter is generally clean and the voice is consistent. A few places that would slow someone down:

- "the hot inward pull each kill had fed beneath his sternum" — "fed beneath" is doing ambiguous double work here. Fed it? Fed from it? The meaning is recoverable from context but a non-native reader might blink twice.
- "Pulse sounded less like a fundamental force and more like something dreamt up in a branding meeting" — "branding meeting" is modern corporate slang that is going to date this sentence and read as a complete non-sequitur to anyone not steeped in office culture. The joke lands for me but it is a narrow target.
- "administratively committed hallucination" — technically "most aggressively committed hallucination in human history, and three cooling bodies on the floor strongly suggested the distinction did not matter" — this whole clause is funny but dense. "Distinction did not matter" is the point, and it is slightly buried. The payoff lands, it just takes a breath.
- "a more sensible version of him might have worried" — the implied grammar here is "a more sensible version of him might have worried about the water being safe." That implied object is doing work that is not on the page. Clear in context, but a reader who is moving fast might miss the beat.

None of these stop the chapter cold. The pacing is fast enough that most readers will roll through without stopping to parse.

## Pacing — Where I Slowed Down, Skipped, or Checked Out

The panic spiral at the start is long. Not bad long — it is the right emotional material for the situation — but around the third paragraph of him cycling through questions and trying to control his breathing, I started wondering when the chapter was going to move. The line "That brought the spiral down from catastrophic to merely vicious" is where it clicked back into motion for me. Everything before that felt slightly overextended. The four questions he lists in italics — *What the fuck was happening? How long had he been here? Why were there monsters in the walls?* — those were good. I just wanted the breathing section to end a little sooner.

The stat block and the analysis that follows it I read fast and enjoyed. The Pulse section especially — the admission that "every kill had fed it" and he had "felt that happen far too clearly to explain it away" — is the kind of LitRPG theorycrafting that I am here for. He thinks like a sensible person about his own numbers. This felt proportionate in length.

The weapon selection section is where the chapter really opens up. I did not skim a single word of it. The hatchet getting put down because of what happens when the enemy gets inside that short arc, the shield being rejected because it wanted too much attention, the spear making sense "immediately" — all of this read at exactly the right pace. I was enjoying every line.

The wound treatment section is appropriately short and I appreciated that it did not overstay. The one beat where the fight flashes back — seeing the first movement from the wall again — is the right length for what it does.

The Agility allocation was slightly flatter than I expected after how well the stat block section worked earlier. The reasoning is tight and correct but I wanted the moment of choosing to feel slightly more decisive. He scratches his jaw, says it will count as a win if it helps even slightly, and selects it. The effect sequence after is good — the "leftover slack drawn in and tied off," the first step landing where he meant it — but the decision beat itself felt like he was filing a form rather than committing to something.

The ending is strong. The arch waking up, the noise from deeper in the ruin, the moment he steps out: clean, propulsive, no hanging around.

## Engagement — What Kept Me Reading, What Almost Lost Me

What kept me reading: the illness relief moment. I said it above and I will say it again. That is the best thing this chapter does and it comes early enough to lock me in. I also stayed for the weapon selection — that section is the most fun I have had reading this story so far. And the spear's system description: "Built for reach, repeat use, and ugly work." Three words that told me everything about what the next fights are going to look like.

The line "You and I have a chance" addressed to the spear would normally make me roll my eyes. It does not here. He is not being romantic about it. He is being practical. The chance he means is real and narrow and he knows it. That distinction carries.

What almost lost me: the Trait section. He stares at *Empty*, the system gives him the same useless two-line answer it gave before, he says "Right. Empty. Brilliant. Not worrying at all." and dismisses it. I understand that is the chapter's honest position — he cannot learn more yet — but we have now had that exact beat twice. The staring at it, the system saying nothing, the dry comment. I wanted him to do something different with it this time even if the result is the same. Maybe the wrong question instead of the same question. Maybe a guess, even an incorrect one, that shows his mind working on it between visits. Right now the *Empty* check-ins feel like a placeholder.

---

## What I Wanted Done Differently

One: I wanted the illness relief moment to go one beat further. He notices his nerves did not betray him, nearly folds from relief, sits down on the bench — and then the chapter immediately moves to breathing exercises and the panic spiral. I wanted him to sit with the illness realisation for one more sentence before the spiral swallows it. That moment is the most emotionally honest thing in the chapter and it gets buried under the next thing almost immediately. Give it two seconds to breathe before you move on. It earned them.

Two: I wanted the Agility allocation to feel more like a wager and less like a sensible administrative decision. He thinks through the logic clearly — he needs to be wrong less often, not survive being wrong more — which is all correct and I agree with him. But I wanted one moment of doubt in there. The near-death, the ribs, the knee, and he still picks Agility over Endurance. That is a real choice and it should feel like one. Even one line of him acknowledging what he is gambling would make it land harder.

---

## Rating

**Would read the next one.**

Adrian picking the spear because it is honest about what it asks of him, the illness relief hitting harder than anything the fight delivered, and an ending that drops a noise from somewhere deeper in the ruin — this chapter made me want to know what is through the arch and I am annoyed that I have to wait to find out.
