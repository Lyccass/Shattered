# Chapter 8 - The Hunt commences

The room started moving before the last line of the prompt had finished settling in Adrian's vision.

Nobody argued with the system. The big man snapped out three short orders in the local language and the others broke in different directions at once. Standing still had become the worst option in the room. The woman in the mended coat went straight to the stores and started checking bundles by touch and count. The archer cleared a strip of floor near the hearth and laid out weapons, spare strings, lamps, and rolled cloth in deliberate rows. The healer ducked through the low back door and came back carrying wrapped linen under one arm and a clay jar under the other. One of the boys dragged a shallow iron tray closer to the fire. Another started feeding wood into the hearth with quick economical movements that said he had done this before and under circumstances he had probably hated.

In less than ten seconds the waystation stopped feeling like shelter and started feeling like a place people were trying to hold through something ugly.

Adrian stayed where he was for one beat too long, spear still in his hand, because none of the jobs came with an obvious slot for the stranger who had appeared off a dead route and then turned out to be Empty.

The woman in the mended coat fixed that by walking up, pushing two empty skins into his chest, and pointing towards the back room.

He looked at Keiran. "Water?"

Keiran gave the smallest nod. "Water."

"I was nearly there."

"I had faith."

The back room held a cistern cut straight into the stone floor and sealed with a fitted slab heavy enough that Adrian had to set the spear aside and get both hands under the lip. The lid came up with a grinding scrape that pulled at his ribs and made his knee threaten complaints at once. He braced it against the wall anyway and crouched over black water that smelled cold, clean, and old. A metal dipper hung from a hook beside the opening. He filled the skins kneeling on one bad leg and one better one, listening to the room beyond him sharpen into faster motion.

When he brought the first pair back, the woman took them without comment, checked the stoppers with her thumb, and pointed at two more. Adrian found he preferred that. It kept the whole thing practical. Gratitude would have turned the work into a favour someone might later decide he owed them for.

The jobs kept finding him after that. He filled more water skins, carried wood from the back wall, helped the healer tear spare cloth into cleaner narrower strips while she sawed through a length of linen with quick hard pulls, and spent a minute sorting lamp wicks by length after somebody shoved a bundle into his hands and pointed at the floor by the hearth. That at least had the courtesy to be a problem with one answer.

What kept catching at him was how ordinary all of it felt. He bent, lifted, turned, crouched, stood, and his body simply followed. Every so often some stupid part of him wanted to stop in the middle of the room and check whether his legs were still working out of pure spite. He kept moving instead. The others did not need that scene out of him, and he did not particularly want to give it to them.

Being useful did not make him trusted. It only gave everyone cleaner reasons to keep him in the room. Each time he crossed near the fire or the stores, somebody's attention snagged on him for half a second longer than it did on anyone else. Empty had not stopped mattering because a bigger problem had arrived. It had simply learned to wait in the corner with the rest of the bad news.

At one point he tried to lash a bundle of cut wood together with the wrong end of the cord and managed such an offensive first knot that the young man who had laughed at the cooking actually stopped beside him and stared. He said something quick and disbelieving. Adrian looked up blankly. The young man made a helpless sound, took the cord from his hands, and retied the whole thing in three fast movements Adrian would have preferred not to examine that closely.

"Right," Adrian said. "Humiliatingly efficient."

The words meant nothing to him, but the tone apparently carried. The young man's mouth twitched before he hefted the bundle and stacked it with the others, and for a few breaths the room loosened around that little embarrassment before tightening again.

Keiran moved through the middle of it without hurrying. He translated when someone needed Adrian to do something more complicated than point and carry, checked the latch on the door twice, looked once through the narrow slit window, and crossed to the hearth to speak quietly with the big man while the others worked around them. Adrian could not decide whether that calm irritated him because it seemed genuine or because Keiran treated looming disaster with the same practical attention he had given a dead boar on a hillside. It made him hard to read and harder to dismiss.

Adrian caught him by the door while the others were still shifting supplies.

"Please tell me we're at least considering not being here when this starts."

Keiran had one hand on the crossbar. "Naturally. Unfortunately, the alternatives look worse."

"And the winning one is?"

"Stay here for the opening. If outside turns out to be merely unpleasant rather than immediately lethal, we can make a better decision then."

Adrian glanced towards the shuttered slit window and then back at him. "That sounds very much like having no idea."

"It sounds like preferring a door and four walls when the system announces a Hunt and declines to define terms."

"You've seen this sort of thing before."

Keiran's fingers stayed resting on the crossbar. "Not this exact event. Similar ones. Enough to know that when the system starts sounding helpful, the part worth worrying about is usually what it chose not to say."

"That is not especially reassuring."

"No," Keiran said. "I was aiming for useful."

Adrian glanced towards the shuttered slit window again. "Can the boundary be tested?"

"Probably." Keiran set the bar properly into place and gave it a brief practical shove. "Whether we should be the ones to test it injured, tired, and in the dark is a different question."

"And if staying in here is the wrong call?"

"Then we learn that quickly, with one wall at our back, instead of halfway across a hillside."

Adrian looked back at the prompt hanging in his vision and disliked how reasonable that sounded.

By the time someone shoved food into his hands, the room looked different from when he had entered. Packs were lined against the wall where they could be seized in the dark without crossing the centre. Water skins stood corked beside the hearth and the back door both. The healer's strips of linen sat rolled and ready in a clay bowl beside the jar of salve. The second fire in the iron tray had been coaxed into a low steady burn, not for warmth so much as insurance. Adrian had seen enough temporary field setups in his old life to recognise the grim logic of one when it formed. This room had been arranged for surviving pressure inside these walls.

The food was a strip of dried meat and something hard and flat that gave under his teeth with obvious resentment. He ate both standing up, because his body wanted fuel more than it wanted dignity and because sitting down felt dangerously close to stopping.

Once the work ran out, nobody relaxed. The woman in the mended coat checked straps a second time. The archer sat by the hearth with his bow across his lap and his fingers resting on the string as if he could feel the countdown through it. One of the boys crouched by the iron tray longer than he needed to after feeding it, looking as though standing up would make the event too real.

Adrian ended up back against the stretch of wall that had become his by default, spear across his knees, the prompt floating where he could not ignore it.

`[System Event Pending: 01:12:44]`

Closing his eyes did not help. The numbers stayed, and the room kept offering up small sounds that made sleep feel ridiculous: cloth dragging over stone, a buckle checked twice, the board by the threshold creaking under the young man on watch, a knife edge whispering over a sharpening stone near the hearth. Once the big man spoke from the back room and got a short answer he clearly had not wanted. After that even the silence felt occupied.

Now and then Adrian caught himself staring at the countdown without meaning to, as if looking harder might change what the system intended. It did not. The numbers kept falling at the same flat, bureaucratic pace.

When they reached ten minutes, nobody had to announce it.

The change in the room came through posture first, with heads lifting and hands stopping halfway through small jobs. The woman in the mended coat held a strap half-threaded through a buckle and did not finish the motion. Even the fire sounded louder for a moment, crackling low in the hearth while nobody spoke.

Keiran crossed the room and stopped in front of Adrian.

"When it starts," he said quietly, "do not try to hold on to anyone. If the event wants separation, it will do it hard."

Adrian looked up at him. "That is an alarming sentence to hear with ten minutes left."

"Then take the useful part," Keiran said.

"That part was clear. Anything more useful?"

Keiran considered that for a beat. "Keep the spear. Move as soon as you land. Get out of the open. Find something with cover before you go looking for anyone else."

Adrian kept his eyes on him. "You make that sound like an order."

"Call it practical advice from someone who would rather not watch you make the obvious mistake."

"And if I happen to land next to whatever the event thinks counts as a predator?"

"Then use the spear immediately and keep moving. The first few seconds will matter more than any plan you come up with after them."

Adrian stared at him for a second. "That is still not a good instruction."

"No," Keiran said. "It is an honest one."

Something shifted at one corner of Keiran's mouth, small enough that Adrian might have imagined it in worse light. "You have done reasonably well so far. I'd hate to see that streak end early."

He moved away before Adrian could ask the obvious follow-up, which was whether the system was about to scatter them by distance, space, or something less reasonable than either.

When the final minute rolled over, sitting there with the spear across his knees felt impossible. Adrian got up around the thirty-second mark and became unpleasantly aware of everything at once: the weight of the shaft across his palms, the pull along his ribs when he breathed too deeply, the healer's hand braced flat against the table, the big man's jaw working once before going still, the young man near the hearth swallowing and then pretending he had not.

The final numbers dropped over the room.

`[00:00:03]`

Keiran was looking past the hearth at something Adrian could not see.

`[00:00:02]`

The fire flattened hard to the left as though a gust had punched through solid stone.

`[00:00:01]`

At one, the room came apart along impossible lines.

The wall opposite Adrian seemed to slide away without moving, dragging everyone in it thinner and farther in the same instant. The big man lurched as if the floor under him had become a slope leading somewhere else. The healer snatched for the edge of the table and missed because the table was suddenly too far to the right. The young man by the hearth met Adrian's eyes for one shocked beat before a white seam split the space between them and folded the room inside out.

Pressure drove through Adrian from teeth to spine. The arch had dropped him through dark and slammed him down at the far end. This dragged him through a gap cut exactly to the shape of his own body while the edges tried to peel him off. Keiran had told him not to hold on to anyone. Adrian only had the spear anyway, and he locked both hands round the shaft hard enough to hurt.

Then stone slammed into his left shoulder and the waystation was gone.

He rolled once on instinct, found one knee under himself, and got the spear up before his eyes had finished catching up to the new dark. Then the size of the place landed all at once.

He was standing on a long platform of worked black stone with a wall of fitted plates on one side and a drop on the other. The ceiling arched high overhead in rib after rib until the dark swallowed the rest. Beyond the platform edge ran two deep channels with a cracked bed between them, like the stripped bones of some transit line too large to fit comfortably under any hill he had seen. Broken housings hung at intervals along the wall. Far ahead and far behind, the channels disappeared into tunnel mouths wide enough to swallow the waystation whole.

Dust lay over most of it in a thick grey film, except where pale blue-white light travelled through seams in the floor and wall. Whatever was feeding that glow, it was not old Earth power. Adrian had given up that lie already.

The air smelled of damp mineral, dead metal, and shut-in cold.

He turned too fast for his shoulder and immediately regretted it.

"Keiran?"

His voice went away down the platform and came back thin and late.

Nobody answered.

He turned farther and found no big man, no healer, no archer, no young idiot with the superior rope knowledge; only the platform, the dead tunnels, a row of cracked marker columns running shoulder-high along the wall, and the slow pulse of that blue-white light under his boots.

The prompt changed before he could swear properly.

`[The Hunt Commences]`

`[Initial Distribution Complete]`

`[Participants have been separated across the active domain.]`

`[Immediate regrouping is not guaranteed.]`

Adrian read that twice because the wording was polite enough to offend him.

The text shifted and resolved into something cleaner.

`[Hunt Panel]`

`Duration: 30 days`

`Event Type: Hunt`

`Completion Criteria: Survive until extraction conditions are met or the event resolves.`

`Event Rules: Initial distribution is mandatory. Boundary traversal denied. Hunt Marks may be earned.`

`Event Information: The active domain contains predators, shelters, transit caches, route anchors, and other participants. Predator pressure and domain instability will intensify over time.`

`Hunt Mark Rules: Gain Hunt Marks through kills, discovery, secured shelter, recovered caches, activated anchors, and completed objectives. Hunt Marks are split among direct contributors.`

`Final Rewards based on Hunt Marks, completed objectives, and extraction state.`

`Current Hunt Marks: 0`

That was better and worse at once.

The system had finally stopped sounding like it was being paid by the clause, but the cleaner version left him with nowhere to hide from the point. He needed to stay alive, keep moving, find shelter and resources before something else did, and accept that anything useful down here would probably be worth fighting over.

Route anchors, transit caches, Hunt Marks. The terms still meant almost nothing, but at least now they sat where he could work at them instead of hiding inside a wall of administrative nonsense.

"Right," Adrian said to the empty platform. "Because being hunted underground apparently needed bookkeeping."

The station gave him the line back a moment later from somewhere in the tunnel and made it sound thinner than he had intended.

Adrian dragged a slower breath in and forced himself to stop reading.

Panic wanted the whole problem at once, but his shoulder hurt where he had landed, his ribs were still there, and his hands were steady enough to hold the spear. The satchel strap had twisted halfway round his chest, and one water skin was still tucked against his side. The knife remained at his belt. The coat, boots, bandages, and salve had all made the jump with him. That was more useful than panic.

He adjusted the satchel strap and hissed when the movement pulled at his side.

"Right," he muttered. "Spear, knife, water, one bad shoulder. Could be worse."

He turned in a slower circle this time and made himself look properly.

To his right, the platform widened where part of the wall had fallen back, exposing a section that might once have been a side chamber or service alcove. To his left stood a bank of old fitted cabinets, most hanging open or half torn free, with a narrow stair dropping below platform level into shadow. Ahead, the channels and the platform ran straight into darkness. Behind him, they did the same. Every direction looked built for movement and long since abandoned by anything sensible.

Nothing about the platform suggested which option counted as less stupid.

He did not need a grand plan yet. He needed to get off the middle of the exposed platform, put stone at his back, and see enough of the place not to walk blind into its first set of teeth. If shelter showed itself, he would take it. If he could learn something without paying in blood, better. Other people could wait until he knew whether being alone was the bigger danger. The event could keep its anchors and marks to itself for another few minutes.

The thought had barely settled when a metallic note rang out ahead of him, too clean to be settling stone.

Adrian froze with the spear already half raised. A second sound followed, lower this time and slower, something dragging or feeling its way along the platform edge in the dark. The blue-white seams under his boots pulsed once, and somewhere up the tunnel something with weight found the stone and kept coming.
