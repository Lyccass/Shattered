# Chapter 4

The last thing kept twitching on the end of the pipe for a second longer than Adrian appreciated. He felt the fight go out of it through his arms before he trusted his eyes. Then the maintenance bay went quiet apart from his breathing and the low hum of the arch somewhere behind the wall. Adrian lowered the thing carefully, because dropping a corpse on his own foot after all that would have been a particularly stupid way to finish the exchange, and stood there with both hands shaking hard enough to make the metal rattle.

There was blood on the pipe, blood on his coat, blood drying across his fingers. Some of it was his. Some of it belonged to the things he had bashed in with a maintenance tool.

"Holy shit," he said, which was about the full extent of his insight.

Then thirst hit hard enough to make his head spin.

His mouth went dry all at once, almost painfully so. Hunger followed close behind, low and mean, and suddenly the room smelled less of blood and old metal than of the fact he had just burned through everything his body had lying around. Apparently the future still expected food and water. Fair enough. Rude, but fair enough.

He sat on the bench because his right knee had started filing formal complaints and looked down at his hands again. The fight replayed in short, ugly bits. Slipping. Losing the line. The knife nearly catching in the sheath on the way out. The first creature on top of him. The second low at his legs. Nothing about it had been clean. Nothing about it had been clever either. He had won because the room was cramped, the creatures were not impossible, and he had been too stubborn to die in the proper order.

Even so, one part would not stop needling at him.

When he had needed his body, it had been there.

It was not graceful or trained or magically competent. But there had been no lag, no dead second, no sly little delay between the decision and the movement. He was bleeding because something had clawed him open, not because his nerves had turned basic motion into a negotiation again. He had spent too long getting used to the opposite to miss what that meant now. Being hurt in a working body was still a bad day. It just was not the same kind of bad.

That thought lodged harder than the claws had. He let it sit there and pulled the status back up before it could turn into something embarrassing.

*[Status]*

*Name: Adrian Keller*  
*Stage: Iron*  
*Level: 2*  
*Class: Fighter*  
*Trait: Empty*  
  
*Strength: 6*  
*Agility: 4*  
*Endurance: 6*  
*Sense: 3*  
*Will: 6*  
*Pulse: 4*  
*Unassigned Points: 1*

He stared at it long enough to remember the last version properly. Level one had been Strength five, Agility three, Endurance five, Sense three, Will six, Pulse three. Now it was six, four, six, three, six, four, with one unassigned point sitting underneath like the system had remembered at the last second that choice was supposed to be part of the appeal.

It was hard not to read the thing like a game screen. Strength. Agility. Endurance. Will. Pulse. Straightforward enough that his first instinct was to distrust it on principle. Games lied all the time. They hid half the mechanics, rounded the numbers, and quietly let formulas do whatever they liked. This one might too. The problem was that he was bleeding on a metal floor with three corpses nearby to compare against, so he could not really afford to treat any line as decorative.

One level was not much data, but it was enough to make a guess. Fighter looked less like a general package and more like a very particular one: strength up, agility up, endurance up, Pulse up with the level itself, then one free point to place wherever you thought your future mistakes were most likely to come from. Sense and Will staying flat fit that. Fighter had not made him broadly better. It had made him more suited to ugly physical problems.

That tracked with the fight a little too well. He had not won because he moved cleanly. He had won because he could still hit hard when the opening appeared, still keep hold of himself after taking damage, and still keep pushing after the exchange should probably have folded him in half. Agility at four was interesting for the opposite reason. He had felt quicker than he used to, yes, but quick and coordinated were not the same thing. A healed body was not the same as a trained one. One let him move. The other would have stopped him nearly dying like an idiot in a maintenance cupboard.

Will sitting at six was almost insulting. Apparently years of hospital corridors and being spoken to like fragile glass had left some useful residue after all.

Pulse remained the awkward one. Four now, up from three, and he had felt something happen when each creature died, that hard inward heat beneath the sternum where the Runeheart sat like a machine somebody had installed without asking. That made Pulse look less like personality and more like energy in the literal sense. Fuel. Spendable somehow. Possibly rechargeable. Possibly not quickly enough to matter if something else came through the wall.

Empty was still there too, which he resented afresh. No Trait, no explanation, no helpful note about what exactly he was missing because apparently the system assumed everybody else had been briefed before arriving. There was also still no Technique on the sheet, which was either normal this early or another place where Empty had left a hole. He did not know which yet. He disliked both options.

The last line sat there with mild, bureaucratic patience.

He focused on it. Another prompt unfolded.

*Assign available stat point?*

At least that answered that much.

He dismissed it again without touching anything. One point might be trivial. One point might matter a lot. He had no scale yet and no intention of spending blind just because a floating menu thought now was a good time. He had already made one life-altering commitment in a sealed room today. One was probably enough.

Then thirst punched back into the conversation and settled the matter for him.

He lowered the screen and looked across the bay. The slumped body by the locker. The empty ration wrappers. The compact lamp. The floor panel he had noticed before the wall-things interrupted matters. If someone had been living out of this space, even badly, there was no chance they had done it with one coat and optimism.

He pushed himself upright and swore under his breath when his ribs answered. The cuts along his left side pulled hot beneath the preservation suit and borrowed coat. His knee had stiffened while he sat. His collarbone stung whenever he rolled his shoulder too far forward. None of it felt disastrous. None of it felt ignorable either. He could work with it. He just was not going to enjoy the process.

The floor panel looked even more used up close. Cleaner metal round the seam. Tool marks along the lip. A recessed notch near one edge that clearly wanted something flatter and more official than a screwdriver jammed in sideways. Adrian took out the narrow metal token he had taken from the dead worker and turned it over under the lamp.

Dark metal. Worn edges. Script he still could not read. Three-point mark in the corner.

He focused on it the way he had focused on the status screen.

Pale text slid into place over the token.

*[Service Token - Proven]*  
*[Access token keyed to maintenance caches and utility locks within a degraded transit cluster. Authority partial. Function unstable.]*

Adrian stared at it for a second, then at the token itself. "Right."

He tried the same thing on the rusted belt knife.

Nothing.

He tried it on the lamp.

Still nothing.

So either he had stumbled into the world's saddest identify skill or the system only bothered when it thought an item was actually worth labelling. That was irritating, but at least it was a rule-shaped irritation rather than complete nonsense. Rare things got a response. Ordinary rubbish had to speak for itself.

He pressed the token into the notch. Something inside the floor clicked and the panel jumped just enough to confirm he was not about to make a fool of himself for no gain.

Close enough.

He got both hands under the lip and hauled it open. The cache beneath was shallow but well organised, fitted between structural ribs and padded with old cloth to stop the contents knocking together. Whoever had built it had known the difference between hiding things and storing them properly.

Water was the first thing he saw and very nearly the only thing that mattered.

He grabbed one sealed bladder, unscrewed the cap with fingers that felt clumsier than they had any right to, and took one swallow that turned into two because his body had clearly decided moderation was for richer people. He made himself stop there. Barely. The water was cold, metallic, and old enough that he probably should have been more suspicious of it. Instead it hit his mouth like theft. He stood there breathing through the urge to drain the rest, took another measured mouthful, then capped it before good sense lost the argument entirely.

The headache behind his eyes eased first. The light-headedness followed. Not gone. Just no longer personally insulting.

Food came next. Four ration bars in dull wrappers stamped with the same script as the token. He focused on one.

Nothing.

Apparently even the future drew a line somewhere.

He opened one anyway. The bar smelled faintly of salt and grain and tasted like somebody had compressed a nutrition lecture into brick form. Adrian nearly laughed on the second bite. It was vile, but it was fuel and his stomach stopped acting like it intended to eat him from the inside. That counted.

Only after water and half a ration bar did he pay proper attention to the rest of the cache, and even then the first thing he saw was not the weapon count so much as the fact somebody had prepared for the world to go bad in a very practical way.

This was not somebody's collection. It was a set of choices.

He found a short sword first because it was nearest to hand. He drew it halfway, felt the balance, and put it back almost at once. Nothing obviously wrong with it. That was the problem. It wanted somebody who knew what a sword was doing in their hand and had some opinion on edge alignment beyond please face the enemy. Adrian currently had no such qualifications.

The hatchet came next and lasted about as long. It felt solid. It also ended every imagined exchange with something close enough to bite him. He put that back too.

The hammer tempted him for a moment longer. It was a brutally honest bit of work, all weight and function, the sort of tool that did not pretend to be subtle about its purpose. Unfortunately it was short, top-heavy, and built for somebody who trusted their footing and their ribs. He trusted neither.

The shield was worse because he genuinely wanted it to be the answer. One more thing between himself and claws sounded lovely. Then he pictured actually managing it. Footwork. Angles. Guard position. Recovery. A whole extra limb's worth of decisions attached to an arm that had already spent part of the day remembering how not to die. No.

His hand found wrapped wood under the rest and stopped.

Of course.

He pulled the spear free far enough to unwrap the head and focused again.

*[March Spear - Proven]*  
*[Field combat spear used along unstable routes and salvage corridors. Built for reach, repeat use, and ugly work.]*

That got a tired smile out of him. "Well. Ugly work I can do."

He lifted it clear of the cache and stood with it in both hands. The shaft was dark wood, scarred and worn smooth where it had been held. The head was plain, leaf-shaped, and very definitely sharpened for use rather than display. More importantly, the weapon made immediate sense in a way the others had not. Reach mattered. Simplicity mattered. The ability to be late, blunt, and still keep a thing away from his organs mattered quite a lot.

He tried one careful thrust in the cramped space. Then another, slower recovery. Clumsy, yes, but not confusing. The weapon did not ask him to be elegant. It asked him to keep the point between himself and the problem and not do anything clever unless he absolutely had to. That sounded about right for now.

The maintenance pipe had already taught him the first half of the lesson. Reach bought time. The spear was just what happened when somebody competent had built the same idea on purpose.

He kept the rusted knife as well, mostly because there were going to be moments when a spear was too much or too slow and dying over a lack of secondary options seemed avoidable. The sword, hatchet, hammer, and shield went back. Not because they were bad. Because he was, at least with them.

The rest of the cache gave up a few more useful things once he stopped looking at it like a starving man. A fresh roll of bandage. A flat tin of bitter-smelling salve. Another water bladder. Spare shafts he left where they were because carrying half the stash on his first walk out would have been optimistic to the point of comedy.

He sat on the bench again and peeled the coat and preservation suit down far enough to look properly at the damage. The three claw lines across his left ribs were the ugliest, shallow but open and angry enough to deserve respect. The collarbone cuts were less serious and more inconvenient. His knee was going purple in a way that promised character-building by evening.

He cleaned what he could with water he resented spending, hissed once when the salve hit the rib wounds, and wrapped himself one-handed with the sort of ugly competence that came from years of improvising field repairs in bad weather. Not medical skill, exactly. More the general principle that if something important was split open you usually started by keeping it shut.

By the time he finished, the wounds still hurt, still pulled, and still made deep breathing feel like a mildly stupid hobby. But they had moved from active problem to managed problem, which was enough for the moment.

The spear lay across his knees. The room smelled appalling. His mouth no longer felt like old paper. His hands had stopped shaking, mostly. Adrian pulled the status back up again.

The numbers had not changed. The free point was still there.

The earlier pattern held. Fighter had given him force, movement, staying power, and left him one point to spend where he thought he would need it. That was a lot clearer now that he had something practical to measure it against. Strength was not his bottleneck. He could hit hard enough already if the line was there. Endurance was earning its keep just fine. Sense had an argument for itself after three wall-creatures and one blind corner, but the spear had made one thing painfully obvious.

His problem was movement.

Not raw speed, necessarily. Not in the sprinting sense. Placement. Recovery. Getting the weapon back in line without tangling himself up. Trusting a step and taking it cleanly. That was where the fight had nearly killed him, and it was also the exact reason the spear appealed. It gave him more room to be a bit late, but it did not solve the lateness.

He focused on the last line.

*Assign point to which stat?*

He considered Will for about half a second before rejecting it on principle. He had no intention of turning years of hospital patience into a build strategy unless he absolutely had to. Sense tempted him. So did another point in Endurance. Both had a case. But if he was honest, the whole reason he had rejected half the stash and kept the spear came down to one thing: he did not yet know how to move this body properly, and until he did, every fight would start in debt.

Agility, then.

He selected it.

The change was nowhere near as violent as class selection or levelling. No crushing pressure, no inward heat, nothing dramatic at all. It felt more like a subtle tightening through the hips and shoulders, a small reduction in slack he had not realised was still there. Adrian stood, rolled one shoulder, shifted the spear from one hand to the other, and took two slow steps across the bay.

Still not skilled. Still not practiced. But the movement came back a fraction cleaner, as if his body had stopped rounding the corners off so badly.

That would do.

He brought the spear up once, thrust towards the far wall, and recovered. Better. Not good. Better.

The ruin picked that moment to remind him it had not paused just because he had been busy sorting himself out.

A heavier vibration rolled through the bay. The lamp on the bench flickered. The red light leaking through the viewing slit shifted towards something colder. Adrian turned at once, spear already in hand, and crossed to the narrow opening in the wall.

The arch chamber had changed while he was drinking, eating, bandaging, and playing accountant with his own stat sheet. Blue-white lines now ran through most of the grooves around the ring, bright enough to reflect off the cracked floor. The dim membrane across its centre had thickened into something almost substantial, not transparent but not opaque either, like frozen water deciding whether to become glass. More troubling than that, a second seam of light had appeared along the far wall where there had been nothing before.

Power returning, then. Doors reacting to it. Possibly things on the other side reacting too.

The token in his pocket had gone faintly warm. Adrian took it out, frowned at it, and looked back through the slit just in time to hear metal strike metal somewhere deeper in the ruin. Not close. Not random either. A second sound followed a moment later, too broken up by distance to make out, but with enough shape to it that "pipes" felt like wishful thinking.

He stood still and listened.

Nothing after that. Just the hum of the arch, the tick and groan of old machinery waking up, and the general sound of a place deciding it was not finished with people yet.

Could have been people. Could have been something else with roughly the same opinion on doors.

Either way, sitting in the maintenance bay with food, water, and one decent spear had stopped being a long-term strategy.

Adrian took one last drink, capped the bladder, and looked from the brightening arch to the lit seam in the far wall.

Then he went to see which problem had opened first.
