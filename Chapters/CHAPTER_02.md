# Chapter 2 - A great Choice

Weight came back first, not pain exactly so much as the sense that something heavy had settled over his lungs and ribs and forgotten to move. Adrian dragged in a breath that tasted of cold metal, dust, and air old enough to seem stored. He opened his eyes to darkness split by a thin red line above him and spent one long second with no idea where he was. Then memory caught up all at once: the cradle, Helix, the blue strip in the lid, the bad bet.

His heart kicked hard enough to hurt. He moved, and his body moved with him. "What the-" he muttered, coughing up a handful of dust.

His right hand snapped up fast enough to surprise him and cracked knuckles-first against the surface above. The pain was immediate, clean, and almost welcome for how simple it was. He flexed his fingers once, twice, then sat there breathing too fast because his hand had gone where he told it to go when he told it to go there, with no lag, no refusal, and no argument from the nerves in between.

For a second he very nearly laughed. For another he very nearly cried. His throat did something he chose not to catalogue. He got on with the more immediate problem of being trapped in a box.

The air smelled wrong. Dead dust. A stale, shut-in smell underneath, the sort rooms got after being sealed too long and opened too rarely. There was no obvious handle inside the cradle, which was about what he would have expected from Helix. He ran both hands along the seam until his fingers found a recessed strip near his left hip. The plastic felt dead and gave nothing. He pressed, dragged, swore at it under his breath, and tried again. Nothing. Whatever release had once lived there had either lost power, seized up, or both.

The lid flexed slightly under his palm, not enough to matter politely. Adrian braced one foot against the inner wall and shoved. Metal shrieked through the dark. He went still. The lid had jumped a fraction upward. He stared at the gap, shoved again, and this time put his back into it. Something brittle tore loose. Sealant snapped with a dry, ugly sound, and the lid lurched high enough for colder air to spill properly across his face.

Dust came with it, along with the smell of old machinery and long-closed space.

He got both hands into the opening, forced it wider, and levered himself upright. For a moment he stayed half folded in the cradle, breathing and not trusting anything yet. Then he swung his legs over the side, and his bare feet hit metal.

Cold climbed straight through him. The floor was subtly tilted beneath his soles, enough that the whole chamber felt as though it had been settling crooked for a very long time. He stood too quickly out of habit and instantly braced for the familiar correction, the little betrayal in his left leg that used to follow any movement made with optimism.

It never came. He waited for it anyway. Still nothing.

Carefully now, as if he might somehow frighten the fact away, Adrian bent one knee, then the other, shifted his weight, dropped into a crouch, and rose again. His body just worked. He touched his forearm, the heel of his hand, then the outside of his left thigh where weakness had first announced itself years ago. Muscle answered under his fingers, solid enough that he had to look away before he did something embarrassing, like start thanking a room.

When he finally looked around properly, the chamber told a bleak enough story by itself.

It had once been clean. That much still showed beneath the ruin. Rows of white preservation cradles sat in recessed bays along both walls, most of them broken open, some half collapsed under fallen panels, and one whole section at the far end crushed beneath a slab of cracked ceiling. Black cable bundles hung down in loops from the ceiling and walls. The thin red line above came from an emergency strip sunk into the ceiling, dim enough to feel less like lighting than a refusal to go fully dark.

Faded lettering ran across the opposite wall.

`CONTINUITY SUITE C`

Beneath it, browned by mineral staining and time, sat the Helix logo.

So that part, at least, had been real.

He stepped away from the cradle and nearly slipped on a drift of pale residue gathered in the floor groove beside it. Dust, mostly, though some of it was finer than it should have been. He glanced towards the nearest open unit and regretted it immediately. Something lay inside — straps, grey residue, and a shape that had once relied on bones.

Adrian looked away at once. He had other problems first.

He moved through the chamber instead, slower now, scanning automatically. Ceiling compromised at the far end. One exit still looked intact. No obvious cameras. No movement. Fresh tracks were harder. He saw none in the dust, but dust settled undisturbed long enough told him less than he wanted.

At the base of one wall panel he found a recessed emergency cabinet. The clear cover had gone cloudy and crazed with age, but when he hit it with the heel of his hand it shattered inward. Inside sat a narrow metal tool, somewhere between a release bar and a bit of industrial optimism. He weighed it in his hand. Light, slightly flexible, and not especially good at anything. At the moment, being good enough at several things would do.

The words came without sound — not in the room but across his vision.

*[Off-cycle life recognised.]*
*[First awakening pending.]*

Adrian stopped so hard the metal bar clicked against the floor. The lines hung in front of him in pale, steady script, not on the wall, not projected into the room, and not floating exactly, just sitting somewhere between sight and certainty, clean enough to read and impossible to place. He shut his eyes. The text stayed — which was not how hallucinations were supposed to work, because hallucinations went away. He opened them again and the text was still there. He took one careful breath.

Right.

So the sedatives had finally found a more imaginative aftereffect. That would have been easier if it had at least tried to be dramatic. Instead the thing carried the tone of a dull administrative notice no one expected to challenge.

"You've got terrible timing," he muttered.

Nothing answered. He moved a hand through the text. No effect. He looked away. The lines lingered at the edge of his vision until he focused again, at which point they sharpened and unfolded.

*[A living adult without awakening has returned to system jurisdiction.]*
*[Initial class selection required.]*

The possibility of waking into jurisdiction had not made the brochure.

A distant impact rolled through the structure beneath his feet, not loud or close but real enough to shake dust off the broken ceiling at the far end of the room. A second later something deeper in the facility answered with a metallic groan so drawn out it sounded less like machinery than a building remembering it still had weight. Hallucination or not, the room had other problems.

He found the intact door in the left wall near the rear of the chamber. The manual release wheel beside it had fused in place under rust and mineral build-up. He set the bar into the spokes and leaned. The wheel resisted, then moved. The second turn came easier. On the third, some internal seal unlatched with a grinding snap, and the door pulled inward far enough to admit a line of colder air.

Adrian slipped through sideways and pulled the bar free behind him.

The corridor beyond had once matched the chamber: white walls, inset lights, rounded corners. Time had not been interested. One wall had split at some point and been repaired with materials that definitely did not come from Helix. Dark fitted plates, almost stone but not quite, had been pinned over the old breach with ribbed black bands. The work was load-bearing and nothing else — the kind of fix you specified when the wall needed to stand and the crew had an hour.

Faded arrows in English still pointed towards `OBSERVATION`, `INTAKE`, and `SECURE STORAGE`. Over them, added later in a sharper hand, ran a second set of symbols Adrian did not recognise. It was writing, not gibberish, and that landed hard enough to dry his mouth on the spot.

Somebody had stood in this corridor long after Helix had finished being Helix and marked the place for other people.

Which meant the people he knew were gone. All of them. His mother included. There was no version of the timeline where she had waited this out and come back to collect him.

He looked at the mineral staining on the old wall, the way the later plates had weathered differently against it, and chose not to finish the estimate. He had made a living reading age and failure off surfaces. He did not want the number.

He had done the arithmetic before he closed the lid. He had sat with it deliberately, calculated it as a condition of the choice, and made the choice anyway. The difference between doing the sum in advance and standing in the result was not something he had a word for yet.

The old instinct to conserve movement caught him standing still for half a second, waiting for fatigue and waiting for the price.

Nothing came.

He went right, following the colder air and what little emergency light remained.

The corridor sloped. Not much, but enough to notice. Thirty metres in, the floor changed under his feet, giving way from the old polished composite to dark fitted plates scored with fine grooves that caught the red light and held it. This was not part of the original Helix build, unless Helix had been hiding a much more interesting business model beneath the client areas. It looked more like transit-linked infrastructure, some heavy service system, or the sort of machine you built beneath a cryonics facility and hoped no customer ever lived long enough to ask about.

Adrian wanted proper lighting, a notebook, and ten quiet minutes.

Instead he got a circular chamber built around a black arch twice his height.

He stopped at once.

The arch itself was not painted black so much as made that way, a segmented ring sunk into a reinforced frame with thick cable trunks disappearing into the surrounding walls. Some bundles had ruptured and spilled glassy fibres across the floor. Others still looked intact. Inspection hatches sat at regular intervals around the inner frame, not hidden and not decorative, just practical access points sized for a person.

The floor at the chamber's centre had cracked into a spiderweb pattern. Dust had gathered heavily there except for one narrow trail of displaced grit leading towards the base of the arch. His first thought was footprints. The second look killed that. No heel marks. No stride. Just a patient, steady drag towards the ring as if vibration had been pulling at the dust, one small increment at a time.

He crouched automatically to inspect it, and the motion still felt obscene in its ease. Two fingers to the plate told him the floor was cold and dry. Beneath that he felt a vibration so faint he might once have missed it entirely, back when his own body had made too much noise to let subtler things in. It was not constant. It pulsed. Measured. Mechanical.

He straightened and looked through the centre of the ring. There was no active light, no visible passage, just dead black, the suggestion of depth, and more of that unfamiliar writing etched into the base frame in shallow repeating clusters. Functional, maybe, or a warning nobody had bothered translating for the dead.

The system reappeared.

*[Initial class selection required.]*
*[Environment unstable.]*
*[Selection may be completed now.]*

Adrian looked up at the arch again. "You think?"

The text ignored him and unfolded the options beneath itself.

*[Fighter]*
*[Ranger]*
*[Caster]*
*[Healer]*

That was the whole list. No explanation of who was offering the choices. No note for the recently thawed.

"Good," Adrian said. "Mysterious bureaucracy. Just what I needed."

He focused on the first option.

*[Fighter]*
*[Direct martial growth. Favours physical reinforcement, close combat, weapon familiarity, and decisive force. Balanced for survival in contested environments.]*

That, at least, sounded honest.

Physical reinforcement meant body first. Close combat meant no pretending distance solved everything. Weapon familiarity sounded broad rather than precious, which helped. Balanced for survival in contested environments was either reassuring or a polite way of saying this was the choice for people expected to get into ugly situations quickly. *Using my body properly for once is indeed very enticing.*

Then he moved to the next option.

*[Ranger]*
*[Favours mobility, perception, skirmish methods, routefinding, and adaptive positioning. Best suited to uncertain terrain, pursuit, and disengagement.]*

That one landed differently. Admittedly, it fit the old version of himself — roads, bad ground, finding the line through a problem rather than through it. For a moment he almost went with it. Then he caught himself: best suited to disengagement was a decent description of the last three years of his life, and he had already spent enough of one life adapting gracefully to smaller options.

He checked the third option.

*[Caster]*
*[Favours Pulse shaping, ranged force, control, and abstract power development. Requires higher precision and grows strongly with system literacy.]*

He had no idea what Pulse was yet, though its appearance in the description confirmed it was something real in the system rather than decorative terminology. A class that required system literacy when he had none was a class built for people who already knew where the floor was.

*[Healer]*
*[Favours restoration, stability, support methods, and preservation under pressure. Excels in attrition, recovery, and group endurance.]*

"So I have four basic MMO starter classes for choice? Great, where is my overpowered Main-Character-Syndrome Class?" Chuckling to himself, he turned back and analysed the options a bit more seriously.

Healer was not happening. Stability had been doctors, handrails, reduced hours, and very sensible language about realistic outcomes, and he had not crawled out of a cryogenic coffin in a dead facility after who knew how many years just to wake up and pick the class whose whole pitch boiled down to hold everything together and help everybody else last longer.

Adrian paced once around the chamber, bare feet nearly silent on the dark plates while the arch hummed very softly behind him. Fighter, Ranger, Caster, Healer. The list was too tidy to be random and too broad to feel personal. More likely it was a standard intake spread — four directions, no overlap. This was probably not a personality quiz so much as a bet on which sort of ignorance he could honestly survive. "On the bright side," he said, "that I am alive even now is already a bonus. I wonder what happened."

The chamber gave another low structural groan, and dust shifted towards the ring. He had already been standing still too long — he stopped in front of the arch and focused on the first option again, already knowing the answer.

*[Fighter]*
*[Direct martial growth. Favours physical reinforcement, close combat, weapon familiarity, and decisive force. Balanced for survival in contested environments.]*

"Fighter," he said.

The word sat there. Nothing changed. Then the prompt shifted.

*[Confirm base class selection: Fighter.]*
*[Yes / No]*

He almost smiled. *Right. Of course.*

He focused on *Yes*.

The world hit him in the chest.

Pressure, sudden and structural, drew inward beneath his sternum hard enough to fold him half forward. Heat flashed through his ribs, down his spine, and out into his limbs. He dropped to one knee on the cracked plate with the metal bar clattering from his hand. This felt more like being assembled under protest.

Lines broke apart across his vision too quickly to read in sequence.

*[Base class selection accepted.]*
*[Runeheart initiation in progress.]*
*[Body-state synchronisation underway.]*
*[Iron stage established.]*

What threw him, oddly enough, was his heartbeat. He had spent years learning the rhythms of a failing body. This was something else entirely, a new pulse catching somewhere deep and hidden and pulling the rest of him into line behind it.

Adrian planted one hand on the floor and forced himself upright. Something inside him had taken shape that had not been there before, or perhaps had always been possible and simply arrived too late for any useful explanation.

The next screen settled into view with unnerving calm.

*[Status]*

*Name: Adrian Keller*
*Stage: Iron*
*Level: 1*
*Class: Fighter*
*Trait: --*

*Strength: 5*
*Agility: 3*
*Endurance: 5*
*Sense: 3*
*Will: 6*
*Pulse: 3*

He read it twice, then a third time more slowly, because if a system insisted on dropping a status sheet in front of him with no manual attached, he could at least try to mug the paperwork. Strength, Agility, Endurance, Sense, Will, Pulse. The first four were obvious enough if the thing was using plain language rather than being cute. Strength was force. Agility was speed and coordination. Endurance probably covered toughness and recovery both — a damage floor more than a ceiling. Sense was perception unless the system had chosen a very stupid synonym for something mystical. Will was harder — discipline, maybe, or honestly something closer to pain tolerance. Some form of internal resistance he didn't have a cleaner word for. It sat highest beside Strength and Endurance, which he chose to treat as flattering until evidence said otherwise.

Pulse was the awkward one. Caster had used the word, and now it sat in the same list as everything else, which made it too central to ignore. Resource, then. Energy, fuel, whatever this world used instead — something the system treated as fundamental and expected him to recognise on sight. Iron sounded less like flavour text and more like the first actual rung of a ladder, while level one confirmed he was standing at the bottom of it.

It was hard not to read the whole thing like a schematic — something that looked legible and turned out to require knowledge he did not yet have. This one had given him a dead facility, an unstable environment warning, and a machine the size of a bus humming quietly in front of him. If trouble followed from that, it was probably not the biggest stretch in the world.

Which raised the question: what was the baseline? How good or bad were his Stats? He definitely didn't feel any different than before. He looked down at himself. Nope, there was definitely no change to be seen anywhere. *I guess this won't stay a mystery for long anyway*. With a shrug, he turned his attention back to the screen in his vision.

The line under Class still looked wrong.

*Trait: --*

He focused on it. The dashes darkened. Then the status rewrote itself.

*[Lineage seed: not found.]*
*[Trait initialisation failed.]*

The space beside his name filled in.

*Trait: Empty*

Adrian stared at the word while the arch gave another low, waking hum behind him.

Everybody else, apparently, was meant to start with something.

He had not.
