# Chapter 3

Adrian kept staring at the word as if that might embarrass it into becoming something else. It did not.

*Trait: Empty*

He focused on it. The rest of the screen dimmed, and two more lines opened beneath in the same dead clerical tone the system had used for everything so far.

*[Trait: Empty]*  
*[No seeded Trait present.]*

He pushed again, half expecting a buried explanation, a warning, or at least the courtesy of a useful definition. Nothing opened. There was no note on what a seeded Trait was meant to do, which was revealing in its own irritating way. The system only omitted definitions when it assumed the term was standard. So a seeded Trait was probably normal, probably expected, and he was the anomaly sitting where the explanation should have been.

Trait had been listed beside Class rather than beneath it, which suggested a second layer rather than a subclass, something separate that was meant to shape the first result. The failure notice had made that uglier, not clearer. *Lineage seed: not found. Trait initialisation failed.* Meaning the system had gone looking for a piece that should have existed and found blank space instead. He had a class he understood in broad terms, numbers he was still trying to map, and a missing component in a place where missing components were rarely decorative. He did not need much more than that to know he had been handed something bad.

"Good," he said into the chamber. "Something else is missing."

The system declined to improve.

The floor shifted under his bare feet with a slow, heavy vibration that came up through the plates rather than across them. Large machinery always had a tone when it started waking properly, even before the noise arrived. Adrian looked back at the arch. Another blue-white line had appeared in the grooves at its base, and while he watched, dust near the centre of the cracked floor slipped another fraction towards the ring.

Empty could wait. The machine probably would not.

He dismissed the screen and started round the chamber again, this time with the arch in mind instead of the word still lodged in his skull like a splinter. The frame had not been built for beauty. That much was obvious. It had been built to survive load, heat, vibration, and the kind of repetitive maintenance you only expected when shutting a thing down was either impossible or catastrophically expensive. Cable trunks disappeared into the surrounding walls in grouped runs instead of neat hidden feeds. Inspection hatches sat at regular intervals around the inner frame, plain and practical. Somebody had expected known failures and built access around them.

One of the lower hatches on the left side sat half behind a support column and a loop of fallen cable. Its rim held a cleaner line than the surrounding frame, and the fasteners at the corners had been turned often enough to break the rust pattern. Not recently, but later than Helix, which did not help much.

He crossed to it, stepped over the cable, and crouched. The hatch had no proper handle, only a recessed slot and two locking pins, one bent and one missing entirely. Adrian set the release bar from the cryostasis room into the slot and levered until the panel gave with a hard metallic crack that bounced around the chamber.

Cold air pressed out across his hands, carrying oil, dust, and the stale mineral stink of closed places that had still been opened often enough to stay dangerous. He checked the arch again before he went in. The blue-white lines at the base had thickened into something closer to current.

That was not comforting.

"Right," he muttered. "Excellent timing."

He ducked through the hatch.

The passage behind the ring curved tight round the back of the arch and looked exactly like what happened when several generations of maintenance crews inherited the same impossible machine and slightly worse budgets. Original structural ribs still carried the load, but between them sat hand-cut patch plates, replacement conduits fixed down with mismatched brackets, and newer weld seams laid over older ones without much concern for neatness. Whoever had worked back here had been keeping a difficult system barely functional and doing it with whatever survived the argument.

Adrian moved sideways through the curved passage with one hand on the wall for balance and the release bar low in the other. Six metres in, the ring widened into a cramped maintenance bay built into the thickness of the support structure, half workshop and half bolt-hole. A narrow viewing slit in one wall gave a restricted angle back into the circular chamber. Opposite it stood three lockers, a bench bolted to the floor, and a rack meant for long-handled tools.

He went to the rack first.

The tool still hanging there was not a weapon unless the person holding it had run out of better options, which, granted, was how most weapons started. It was a dark maintenance pipe, chest-high on him, with one end fitted to a bent triangular housing where some removable attachment had once locked in. Too much weight sat near the grip, and too much flex ran through the shaft. The metal head had gone dull and slightly crooked from years of practical misuse.

When he fixed his attention on it, a line of text slid into view.

*[Maintenance Pipe - Inferior Quality]*  
*[Long-reach inspection tool. Bent head. Poor balance.]*

More helpful than most of the system so far.

He tested the weight once, then braced the metal head against the bench edge and worked the bend back as far as the old metal would allow. It was still not good, but it was less offensive than before. Reach was reach, and at the moment he would take structural advantage in whatever shape it came.

The first locker had fused shut. The second opened onto cleaning supplies, two cracked masks, a coil of cable, and a sealed canister he left alone on principle. The third had been forced at some point and never repaired properly. He pulled it open and found layers of somebody else's interrupted life: an insulated work coat, old but intact at the seams; a pair of dark boots with stiff leather and serviceable soles; two empty ration wrappers covered in the same unfamiliar script as the marks in the corridor; a compact lamp with no obvious switch; and a roll of bandage, brown at one end and gone hard in the middle.

And behind the boots, half hidden in shadow at the base of the locker, was a hand.

Adrian stopped and looked properly.

The rest of the body sat slumped in the narrow dead space between locker and wall where it had collapsed and stayed. Cloth had gone stiff and grimy. Leather straps still crossed what had once been a chest. The skull tilted into the corner. One leg had been pinned under the shifted locker when it moved, which explained why the body had not fallen farther. It was not Helix-old but later than that, later enough for the coat, later enough for the ration wrappers, and later enough that this bay had been used by somebody who knew what it was for.

He set the pipe against the bench and crouched, keeping his weight on his toes. There was no obvious killing wound and no major break suggesting a fight. The stained bandage and the posture of the body suggested something meaner and simpler: injury, exhaustion, and a problem that had outlasted the person trying to fix it.

He checked the pockets anyway because surviving the dead was still part of surviving. A rusted belt knife in a cracked sheath. A narrow metal token punched with the same script as the wrappers and marked with a three-point symbol in one corner. A laminated scrap with more writing on it, stiff enough to survive and useless enough to keep for later. He took the knife and the token and left the rest where it was.

The boots were half a size too large, which was still better than bare feet on cold metal plates in a place that kept shuddering under him. He sat, pulled them on, and laced them tightly enough to kill the heel slip. The coat came next. It smelled of old dust, trapped sweat, and the particular dry cold that got into fabric and never entirely left. One sleeve had been patched twice, but warmth was warmth and he was in no position to be choosy.

He rolled the cuffs once, picked up the lamp, and turned it in his hands until he found a pressure strip tucked into the grip. A flat white beam snapped on hard enough to make him squint.

The room sharpened at once. Scrape marks on the bench. Repeated tool scoring near a recessed floor panel. Thin dark smears near the viewing slit, dragged rather than splashed. When he lowered the beam, he found tracks in the dust between locker and wall, then more at the base of the buckled panel in the back corner.

They were not boot prints.

Four close-set claw marks and a dragged centre line crossed the dust again and again, low to the ground.

He followed them to the panel seam and killed the lamp immediately.

Darkness came back thick and close. Adrian stood still with the pipe angled forward and waited through three slow breaths. The bay held silence just long enough to feel deliberate. Then something scraped from behind the wall, dry and light and too regular for settling. A second scrape answered farther left.

Small, then.

And definitely more than one.

The floor vibrated again, deeper this time. The arch beyond the wall answered with a low hum that settled into the plates under his boots and stayed there. The scraping quickened.

Adrian backed towards the open hatch. The room was too narrow to swing well and too cluttered to retreat cleanly. The bench hemmed in his right, the lockers crowded his left, and the buckled panel ruined the footing in the back corner. If something came through fast, reach would matter for one move and then the room would take it away again.

The first creature forced itself out under the panel in a pale rush of joints and hooked claws. It was about the size of a large dog, if a dog had been designed by somebody who disliked the concept. Hairless hide stretched tight over a lean frame. Four legs and no visible eyes. The head narrowed to a wedge of bone and cartilage with a mouth set too far back, opening sideways more than down. It paused half clear of the wall and worked its jaw at the air.

So not sight, then. Vibration, smell, or something worse.

The creature turned towards him with immediate certainty and launched.

Adrian thrust the pipe with both hands. The angle was wrong and the shaft flexed under the impact, but the metal head caught it high in the chest and knocked it off line far enough that it hit him with shoulder and claws instead of teeth. The force drove him back into the bench. One hooked claw tore through the borrowed coat at the collarbone before he managed to shove it away.

It hit the floor, twisted, and came back much faster than something that wrong had any right to move. A second shape came through the seam behind it. Adrian stepped to keep both in front of him and clipped the bench leg with his heel. The half-stumble cost him the clean line. He got the pipe across the first creature's body, got the bent head into something soft, and lost the advantage at once when the second one shot low past the shaft and hit his shin hard enough to take his leg out from under him.

The floor came up hard in metal, dust, and grit.

He kept hold of the pipe for about half a second. Then the first creature was on him, claws punching through coat and then skin, the shaft trapped useless across his chest between their bodies. The knife was at his belt. He got one forearm up under the creature's jaw and dragged the blade free with his other hand by feel. The knife caught on the sheath lip on the way out and nearly slipped. Then the thing drove down again with that tearing-cable scream beside his ear, and Adrian rammed the blade up under the ribs where there was space.

Warm liquid hit his hand immediately.

The creature convulsed. He stabbed again because the first attempt had never solved a problem in his life and there was no reason it would start now. The second strike went in badly. The third found something deeper. The body on top of him kicked once, hard enough to jar his shoulder, then sagged in a full heavy collapse with no grace in it.

He lay there pinned under the weight for one long second, knife still in his hand, blood running over his fingers and into the sleeve of the coat. The smell came late, copper and rot with something sour and old underneath, and then the Runeheart hit.

A hard pulse struck under his sternum and spread inward, hot and wrong, not through the skin but through the new structure that had taken root there. It felt like heated grit driving into the centre of his chest and staying there. His breath locked. The dead creature was still on him. He could not tell whether the heat had come from it or from him or whether there was any useful difference between those options.

The second creature was still alive. It came in from his left while his lungs were still deciding whether to cooperate. Adrian rolled the dead weight off himself, barely cleared the next line of claws, and took the hit across his ribs instead of his throat. Pain flared sharp and immediate. He kicked out on instinct, caught the thing somewhere in the shoulder, and bought enough room to get one knee under him.

The pipe lay against the bench. He grabbed it, shoved himself upright, and nearly went down again when his right knee objected under the sudden load. The creature circled low, jaw working, head lifting whenever his boots scraped against the floor as if sound itself drew lines it could follow. The first one, the one he had punched through the chest earlier, was moving more carefully now. Dark fluid ran down one foreleg.

Hurt was still hurt here.

The room had somehow become smaller than it already was. There was a dead thing on the floor, a bench at his right hip, lockers to his left, and an open hatch behind him that he could only reach by turning sideways and giving the room his back for one second too long.

The wounded creature came first. Adrian shortened his grip and drove the pipe down at its head. The strike landed badly, more shaft than metal, but it forced the thing lower and into the bench instead of into his legs. He shoved with both hands, used the bench as the hard surface the room had denied him, and pinned the creature between the metal leg and the floor while it thrashed in a blind frenzy of claws and blood.

It took longer than he wanted, long enough for both shoulders to start shaking under the strain. The creature hammered the front of his coat, scraped his boot, then weakened by degrees into smaller convulsions and finally stopped.

The second pulse hit under his sternum as the body went slack, smaller than the first and deeper, as if the hot pressure had found somewhere to settle this time instead of merely striking and dispersing.

Adrian took one step back and nearly tripped over the first corpse because his arms had not yet accepted that the fight had changed shape again. The last creature had retreated towards the lockers. It stood there with its head tilted and its sideways jaw flexing in small, deliberate motions. Blood from the dead ones had changed the air. So had his. It paced once, stopped, and turned towards the scrape of his boot sole on the plate.

So it really was tracking vibration.

Useful to know. Late, but useful.

Adrian drew one slow breath, then another, and forced himself not to adjust his stance once he had it. The pipe stayed low and forward, the bent head placed where it might catch something coming fast. Warmth ran down his left side from the ribs. The cuts across his collarbone burned when he tightened his grip. The whole bay hummed around him.

The creature launched at the first real sound he made, a boot edge slipping half an inch on the dust-dark floor.

Adrian drove the pipe point first.

The hit landed lower than he wanted, under the chest instead of through the throat, but the creature had committed and the bad angle mattered less than the fact there was nowhere left for it to go. Its weight slammed down the shaft and through both his arms hard enough to buckle his elbows. He gave ground anyway, one step and then another, until the rear end of the pipe hit the bench edge and stopped there with all the ugly leverage the room had refused him earlier.

The creature thrashed against the metal head. Claws scraped over his boots. Hot blood ran down the shaft onto his hands. He kept both hands locked on the grip and leaned until his whole body started shaking with the effort. The thing fought for several seconds after the outcome had become obvious, which he resented on principle.

When it finally went still, silence came back all at once.

The third pulse did not strike. It drew inward instead. Heat moved through the centre of him and sank into the Runeheart with the slow certainty of something being claimed rather than merely received.

Adrian stood there with a dead creature pinned to the end of an inferior maintenance pipe and did not move for a moment because movement seemed, frankly, a bit ambitious.

Then the corpse twitched once more and his hands started shaking.

He lowered it to the floor more carefully than he had lowered himself into cryostasis and stepped back until the bench hit the backs of his knees. The room smelled like opened meat, machine oil, and old dust. Up close, the creatures looked worse. Thin scar tissue clustered round the jaw hinges. The rib membranes had a stretched, unfinished quality to them, as if they belonged to some other design that had given up halfway. Blood from the first kill had dried tacky over his fingers where the knife had trapped it against his palm.

The system flared across his vision.

*[Stage: Iron. Level 2.]*

Adrian breathed out through his nose and tightened his grip on the pipe rather than opening the rest just yet.

Somewhere beyond the wall, the arch's hum deepened.
